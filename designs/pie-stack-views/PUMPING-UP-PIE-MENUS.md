# Pumping Up Pie Menus

*Don Hopkins · August 2026*

**Thesis:** Continuous, reversible zoom — visual, semantic, and layout at once — lets a pie menu be not merely popped up but *pumped up*: radial distance as an expression of appetite, the center as a round HyperCard stack, and detail accreting around the point of attention like Amsterdam rising around de Dam.

Part of the **pie-stack-views** design cluster ([README](README.md)). Critique origin: [DYE-A-TRIBE](../DYE-A-TRIBE.md). The data model is [Sparse View Overlays](SPARSE-VIEW-OVERLAYS.md).

---

## Semantic zooming

The overlay's parameters are not only visual — view scale, child scale factor — but semantic: a **summary scale** that selects how a node describes itself at its current size, stepping down from a short description to a one-sentence identifier to a tab label to a few iconic emoji to a single emoji, generated on demand by a language model. The zoomable-interface lineage — Perlin and Fox's Pad, Bederson and Hollan's Pad++, and Bederson's later Jazz and Piccolo toolkits at HCIL — named this **semantic zooming**; an LLM makes the semantics generative rather than hand-authored. Zooming becomes level-of-detail in meaning as well as in pixels.

## Distance as appetite

Radial distance already carries leverage — arc length grows with radius, so farther out buys finer angular precision from the same motor accuracy ([Reselection](RESELECTION.md)). It can also carry appetite. Moving out into a slice is itself an expression of intent: *more, about this*. The display can answer by pushing aside the fog of war — the center smoothly refocuses on the current item, opening detail as the pointer moves farther out and folding it away, reversibly, on the way back in.

Appetite is not the only thing radius can carry, and the meanings do not mix: an *operational* pie can spend the same dimension on **scope** — how many objects the verb reaches — which is worked out in [The Window Resize Pie](WINDOW-RESIZE-PIE.md#radius-now-means-three-things-and-they-must-not-collide). Appetite belongs to navigational menus where the item is a place; scope belongs to menus where the item is a verb. A menu claiming both at once is ambiguous.

The model to aspire to is the computer-animated history of Amsterdam rising around de Dam: the city accretes outward in ordered rings, each century growing up in place around the point you are watching. Pumping outward should feel like that — detail building up around the current item in layers that stay navigable like a subway map, outer city and suburbs of information appearing in the order that they matter, not as a data dump.

## The public square

The city metaphor pays one more dividend: the center is the public square — it is where you start out — and the user can drag far-off useful places in from the outskirts and install them there, then save and restore multiple public-circle configurations. Call them modes (god forbid — Tesler drove to work under a NOMODES license plate) or tools (goddess forbid); either way they are saved arrangements of the starting circle, and they need not be switched by hand: schedules, state machines, or DWIM engines can orchestrate which circle greets you, so long as the user can always see which one did and override it.

## The Atkinstown stack

The thing in the middle deserves a shameless name: it is a **stack** — a round HyperCard stack, in tribute to Bill Atkinson. Any mapping between menu and stack can be supported, but one deserves to be the built-in default:

**The stack is the pie menu. A background is a slice. A card is an item.**

The isomorphism is exact. A slice holds zero or more items as a background holds zero or more cards; the items in a slice share that slice's chrome and behavior as cards share a background; and messages pass item to slice to menu exactly as Atkinson routed them card to background to stack. With that mapping the tracking is automatic: highlighting an item shows its card over its slice's background, an empty slice still presents its background, and the neutral center shows the stack's home card. Authoring reduces to adding the backgrounds and cards you want in the editor; saved public-square configurations are simply stacks, exchanged whole. The square is Atkinstown, and the model already proved that ordinary people will author with it.

(One honest seam: in HyperCard a background cannot exist without at least one card. The menu mapping relaxes that constraint deliberately, because empty slices are legitimate.)

Self-revelation gains an authoring story in the bargain: documenting a pie menu means a card for each item, auto-generated by default and hand-built where it matters — and since cards are cards, the documentation can contain live interactive elements rather than screenshots of them.

## The pump

New controls can condense in the center as an item takes focus, so the user can return to the middle and operate the interface they just opened. Pumping in and out along a slice's spoke can page through successive levels of detail; a single stroke out and back is a natural *be there* gesture — arrive at the item, and a fresh circle of options rises around it, which is the memory-palace navigation ([Pie Menu Memory Palaces](PIE-MENU-MEMORY-PALACES.md)) expressed as a pump instead of a glide.

These interactions can be expressed many ways, and a pie menu component worth its crust does not hard-code the choice: it supports them through callbacks, robust dynamic APIs, and live feedback channels, because the center-feedback show a web browser can stage differs radically from what a Unity3D scene can — the contract has to be abstract enough to survive both.

## The Dasher pivot

Navigation should also pivot, reversibly, into **Dasher space**. Ward, Blackwell, and MacKay's Dasher (UIST 2000) steered text entry by continuous zooming: the options ahead are nested boxes, sized by a language model's probabilities, and pointing toward one grows it until you are inside it — selection by arrival, with mid-course correction available the entire way. That is reselection expressed as pure navigation: there is no commit event at all until you have visibly, reversibly flown into your choice.

A pie menu graph admits the same projection. Turn the current circle's slices into the column of boxes ahead — sized by probability, recency, or dwell history — and steer into them, several levels deep in one continuous motion; pivot back to the radial view whenever direction and rehearsal serve better. The two views trade strengths: the pie gives rehearsable motor memory and equal-opportunity visibility, Dasher gives model-weighted lookahead and eyes-on continuous entry. The sparse view overlay does not care, because both are views of the same structure — pivoting between them is the same move as pivoting between any two views of one graph.

Dasher also settles an argument in advance: its box sizes were language-model probabilities in 2000. Model-weighted interface geometry is not an LLM-era novelty; the generative summary scale is the same idea with a bigger model, and it inherits the same obligation Dasher met — the model proposes the sizes, but the user steers.

The pivot has a continuous cousin: the layout can also be *dialed* between radial pie tree and linear outliner rather than switched, with different kinds of children opening in different directions or facets along the way — see [Sparse View Overlays](SPARSE-VIEW-OVERLAYS.md), where projection is treated as just another overlay parameter.

### Every cursor carries its own model, so personality becomes geometry

If box sizes are language-model probabilities, then **the model is a per-cursor possession, not a
system setting.** Each [reading cursor](../webtop/READING-CURSORS.md) owns its own Dasher profile:
the flame cursor dashes and autocompletes into flamier linguistic space than the math cursor does,
because a different model is sizing the boxes ahead of it.

This is what makes character more than skinning, and it is worth stating as the technical claim it
is: **personality stops being a label on the cursor and becomes the curvature of the space around
it.** You do not read that a cursor is Hunter S. Thompson; you feel it, as which directions are wide
and easy to steer into and which require precision. Two cursors on the same page are navigating
differently-shaped continuations of the same text. A Thompson cursor and a Minsky cursor take
different notes because the cheap moves are in different places.

And it composes, because there are two conditioning sources: **where you are** and **who you are.**
A cursor standing in a corpus can have its model conditioned on that corpus — autocompleting in the
local dialect of wherever it happens to be — while its own character profile weights the same space
toward what it finds interesting. Position × personality = geometry.

Three-way convergence worth noting, since two of the three are already in this repo: **Sims
advertisement scores, Dasher box sizes, and pie slice widths are the same quantity** — target size
proportional to expected value *to this agent*. An object broadcasting how good it is for you, a
box sized by how likely you are to want it, and a slice sized by how often you pick it are one
mechanism wearing three interface costumes. Which also means the [dwell-time
prediction](../webtop/EBIKE-PATH-GRAMMAR.md#the-receipt-selkers-dwell-time-and-pie-menus) already
built into pie menus is a language model over choices; it just has not been asked to size anything
yet.

MacKay's own framing is the destination: *"Writing is navigating in the library of all possible
books."* Every book in Borges' library is reachable in Dasher — improbable text is a **small target,
not an absent one** — and the model only decides what is easy. That is the property to preserve when
scaling this up, because it is exactly what autocomplete-with-a-top-five-dropdown throws away.

**The honest conflict, which the pivot framing above softens too much.** Pie menus work because the
geometry is *stable*: the same gesture always means the same thing, which is what makes rehearsal and
mousing-ahead possible. Dasher's geometry is *probability-driven and therefore changes constantly*.
You cannot have both in one view — a probability-weighted pie menu is a pie menu with its principal
virtue removed, and no amount of smooth animation fixes that.

The resolution is a boundary rather than a blend: **keep the rungs where muscle memory operates
geometrically fixed, and let the model shape only the rungs below it** — the open-ended
continuation space where the vocabulary is unbounded and there was never anything to rehearse. Fixed
commands up top, modeled text underneath, and the seam falls exactly where the vocabulary stops being
a closed set. That is a testable line, and it is where the two interfaces should meet.
