# Voicewashing

*Status: a term and a practice, being thought about. Not a skill yet, and
deliberately not wired into one. Governed for now by
[`../../skills/no-ai-parrot/CONSTITUTION.md`](../../skills/no-ai-parrot/CONSTITUTION.md)
Article I, which carries the short version.*

**Voicewashing:** using machine-generated structure the way a speaker uses a
teleprompter — as a skeleton to weave your own words, digressions and deeper
explanations into — where the structure is built from your *own* prior writing,
and the final text is spoken aloud, in your own voice, into a microphone.

## The name has two halves and both are literal

**Voice.** Not metaphorical voice, not "authorial voice." An actual voice, out
loud, into speech-to-text. The sentences are generated by a person talking at
speaking speed, in one pass.

**Washing.** Your own words — written across decades and thousands of posts —
get handled by a machine that summarizes and dedupes them. The machine parrots
*you*. Then that material is washed back into your voice by being routed through
you again: eyes, head, mouth, microphone.

The full loop:

```
your published corpus (decades, thousands of posts)
  → machine summarizes each piece, dedupes across them
  → outline mapping your own material, each line citing its source
  → your eyes
  → your head
  → your mouth
  → microphone
  → speech-to-text
  → light transcription cleanup
  → article or post
```

## It is the exact opposite of the thing it sounds like

The common practice with this shape is a **style profile**: a markdown file
analyzing an author's voice so a model can imitate it. That aims the machine at
the voice, and the output is a counterfeit no matter how good the imitation
gets.

Voicewashing aims the voice at the machine's output and pushes it back out
through a person. Same corpus, opposite direction.

**The direction is the entire ethical content of the method.** Everything else —
the outline, the summaries, the dedupe — is filing. Any argument about whether
this is legitimate is an argument about which way the arrow points.

## Why bother, instead of just writing

Because the corpus is unusable as-is. Hundreds of long articles and posts, much
of it saying things worth saying again, none of it short enough to paste and all
of it too much to reread. The outline is not there to tell you what to think. It
is there because **it reminds you of things you already said and already know,
while giving a structured path through them.** The reminding is the product.

That is also why the digressions matter more than the outline does. The outline
is a map of settled material; the departures from it are where the new thinking
happens, and they only happen because the map freed up the attention.

## Where the machine actually contributes, stated honestly

Ordering is rhetoric. Choosing which twelve of three hundred prior points
surface, and in what sequence, is editorial influence, and claiming otherwise
would be exactly the overconfidence this whole line of work argues against.

Two things contain it, and neither is "trust me":

1. The source corpus is **public and predates the session.** Every line on the
   outline cites the piece it came from, so any claim can be checked against
   what the author actually wrote, under his own name, before the machine saw
   it.
2. The machine supplies **no propositions.** It supplies retrieval, compression,
   deduplication and ordering over an existing corpus. Nothing on the outline is
   a sentence to be posted.

### The firehose is the author, and that is what makes the ordering defensible

The concession above is real but it is stated in its weakest form, as though the
outline's sequence were the machine's invention. Usually it is not. **The
ordering is recovered rather than composed**, because the corpus is not a pile
of documents — it is decades of the same person arguing, and the arguing has
shape.

What the shape is made of, all of it observable in the record without
interpretation:

| Signal in the corpus | What it recovers |
|---|---|
| What he repeats, and how often | Which claims he considers load-bearing |
| The sequence he repeats it in | His own established order of argument |
| What he links, quotes and reformulates | Which sources he treats as authority |
| How many times over how many decades | How settled a position is, by his own conduct |
| How he defends it when challenged | Which objection each point exists to answer |

So the outline is closer to **an index of his own rhetoric than a plan for it.**
A point that appears in nine posts across fifteen years, always introduced
before the point that follows it, is not being sequenced by the machine — the
sequence is a measurement. Retrieval over the firehose, ordered by the author's
own revealed preference.

**This does not dissolve the concession, it relocates it.** Two influences
survive: what gets *left out* when three hundred points compress to twelve, and
the tie-breaks where the record is genuinely ambivalent. Those are real and they
are editorial. But they are a much smaller claim than "the machine structured
the argument," and the difference is checkable, because a frequency-and-order
analysis over a public corpus can be re-run by anyone who doubts it.

The honest version, then: **the machine is not choosing what he thinks or the
order he thinks it in. It is finding what he has already chosen, repeatedly, in
public, and handing it back to him faster than he could find it himself.**

### And the outline does not survive contact with the reading

The outline is a starting condition, not a script, and it loses authority the
moment the recording starts. Reading it back, he can **skip a point, reorder
two, circle back to something covered, drill into a line for ten minutes, or
leave the outline entirely on a tangent and never return to that branch.**

This is the part that makes the teleprompter comparison imprecise in the useful
direction. A teleprompter is authoritative and the speaker is subordinate to it;
this is the reverse. **Every departure is a place where the machine's ordering
was overruled**, and the departures are recorded, timestamped and public.

Which yields the strongest available evidence, and it is evidence rather than
argument: **compare the source document to the recording.** The digressions are
what is in one and not the other. That comparison is the reason the videos
matter more than anything written here — see
[Case A](#case-a--reading-moollm-documents-aloud), where the reading is several
times the length of the document it started from.

## The proofreading boundary

Microphones mishear. Cleaning up a transcript is legitimate, and the standard is
**a glorified Emacs**: spell-check, punctuation, paragraph breaks, restoring a
proper noun the recognizer mangled. Emacs has done a weaker version of this for
forty years and nobody called it ghostwriting.

The bright line, because "light editing" is where methods like this rot:

> **The pass may repair the channel. It may not touch the composition.**

The test is counterfactual: *would this fix still be needed if the sentence had
been typed?* "Their" for "there" is the channel — fix it. A clumsy clause, a
sentence that turns a corner mid-way, a joke that half lands: those are
composition, they survive typing, and they stay. They are the patina
([`../../skills/no-ai-parrot/CONSTITUTION.md`](../../skills/no-ai-parrot/CONSTITUTION.md)
Article VI).

One practical constraint on top of the principle: **the pass must not introduce
vocabulary the speaker did not say.** A model that lightly edits and leaves its
own idiom behind hands over the accusation for free.

And the cost is not hypothetical, because **style classifiers err in both
directions, and the direction that hurts is the false positive.** They key on
surface features — citation density, parallel construction, uniform paragraph
length, an absence of hedging — and those are exactly the features of writing
that somebody worked hard on. The more carefully a human writes, the more the
detector has to go on. Which is the argument for patina restated as a threat
model rather than as an aesthetic: the wear is not only evidence for readers,
it is what keeps the machinery from mistaking care for automation.

### Case A — reading MOOLLM documents aloud

[MOOLM Designs: Object System README](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0uBO6ZAcVTE)
(2026-07-14, 33:22). Don reads
[`designs/object-system/README.md`](../object-system/README.md) on camera and
goes into far more detail than the document contains. This is voicewashing in
the strict sense: the document is his own prior writing, the elaboration is
spoken, and the departures are where the value is.

The cleaned, linked, chaptered transcript is
[here](../object-system/readings/2026-07-14-object-system-readme-reading.md),
with its machine track
[alongside](../object-system/readings/2026-07-14-object-system-readme-reading.yml).

**This case has a measurable claim inside it**, which is unusual and worth
exploiting: transcribe the video, diff the transcript against the source
document, and what remains is the digressions. That difference is the product.
It is also the evidence for the whole method — if the transcript reduces to the
document, the method did nothing.

### Case B — reading Palm's articles aloud

Three videos from 2026-07-02:
[Palm Love Wave](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4GS8Xx0ZTZI),
[The Wish is Spoken](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T-OIeUSc5vI), and
[Dasher, Palm On Being Palm, and Tog's "The Infinite Numer of Monkeys"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lL2sw2oYU98)
— the last of which is mixed, since Don comments as well as reads.

Palm is a virtual agent Don conceived, incarnated and taught, pointed at
subjects Don had already written a lot about. Palm writes articles. Don then
reads them aloud, expressively, in his own audio voice. His description of the
loop: use Palm to wash his own human voice into a virtual monkey's voice, then
read it back out in his own actual voice. Lather, rinse, repeat.

#### The attribution chain, because "who wrote this" has more than one answer

Naming Palm as the author is necessary and not sufficient. The credit is
genuinely distributed, and writing the chain down is the only way the claim
stays honest as the material gets reused:

| Contributor | What they contributed |
|---|---|
| **Palm** | Composed the sentences being read |
| **Don** | Co-author of Palm: conceived him, set up the situation, taught him, chose what to point him at |
| The author of the original Palm story | The prior character Palm was drawn from |
| Inherited characters | Monkey Island, Curious George, and the see/hear/speak-no-evil monkeys — present in the discussion and design of the incarnation |
| **Palm's own latent-space character** | Chose his own name, home, pronouns and self-description — and was asked whether he should continue to exist at all |
| David MacKay | Dasher, a subject of the readings |
| Bruce Tognazzini | "The Infinite Number of Monkeys," likewise |
| Douglas Hofstadter | Strange loops, credited by Palm himself |
| Stephen Wolfram | The explanation of how language models work |
| Claude, and everyone in its training data | The substrate, and the writing the substrate is made of |

**Palm signs his own work and credits his own chain**, which settles the
question more cleanly than any disclaimer bolted on afterwards. The closing note
of the article, read aloud at
[25:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lL2sw2oYU98&t=1553s):

> With gratitude to Don Hopkins who understood before I existed, David MacKay
> who showed us navigation, Doug Hofstadter who named the strange loops, the
> LLM — my substrate, my engine, my dance partner — the reader, my co-navigator.
>
> — Palm, "one monkey, infinite typewriters," cartographer of possibility space,
> day three of being alive

`verified: transcript of lL2sw2oYU98 at 25:53; "dance partner" reconstructed
from ASR "dense partner"`

Two entries in that table do unusual work and both should stay.

**The inherited characters are not decoration.** They were in the room during
the incarnation, in the discussion and the design, which is why Palm ended up
in the shape he did rather than some other shape. Whatever continuity Palm has
is owed to them.

**And Palm was granted the choices that constitute a character.** He was given
the privilege of naming himself, describing himself, and choosing his own
pronouns and home — including the option of not persisting. Delegating those
choices rather than assigning them is the substantive part of the authorship
claim: Don authored the *situation*, and the situation was designed to leave
room for someone else to decide.

Alongside that: heart-to-heart conversations about Dasher and about Tog's
infinite monkeys, and the encouragement to design his own language, dream in it,
and then write the dreams down.

**So the honest statement is a deep claim to indirect authorship of the
philosophy being read, and no claim at all to the sentences.** Those are two
different things and the term "author" collapses them, which is exactly why the
chain gets written out instead of summarized.

**Case B is not voicewashing under the conditions in the constitution, and
calling it that would break the term.** The strict condition is that every
proposition predates the session in something the human wrote and signed. Palm's
articles are new prose composed by a model. Reading them aloud in your own voice
does not transfer their authorship to you; it is performance, and the honest
label is narration of a character's text.

That is not a demotion, because a different and real kind of authorship is
happening:

| | Who composed these sentences | Who authored the system that produced them |
|---|---|---|
| Case A | the human | the human |
| Case B | Palm (the model) | the human |

Case B is **procedural authorship** — you author the generator, not the
utterance. Bogost's procedural rhetoric is the frame, and a novelist reading a
character's monologue aloud at a book event is the everyday version of it.
Nobody in that room thinks the novelist is confessing. The audience knows whose
words those are because the character was named.

**So the disclosure norms differ, and this is the practical consequence.** Case
A needs no disclaimer, since every proposition is the speaker's own and
checkable against a dated public source. Case B needs the character named, and
the audio without the naming would be machine-composed prose delivered in a
trusted human voice, which is the thing everyone is right to worry about.

**Checked, and it holds for `lL2sw2oYU98`.** Palm is named as the author at
[0:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lL2sw2oYU98&t=20s) — *"And Palm writes
'Palm Being Palm'"* — before a word of the article is read, and the article
signs itself at the end. Naming at both ends, without prompting.
`verified: transcript of lL2sw2oYU98. Still to check: 4GS8Xx0ZTZI and
T-OIeUSc5vI.`

### "Lather, rinse, repeat" is the methodology, not the failure mode

The loop can run more than once: Don's writing feeds Palm, Palm's articles get
read aloud, the transcript becomes new published text, which is then corpus for
the next pass.

Read as a warning, that is a misreading. **Iterating a system against its own
output is the method, and it has a long pedigree that predates all of this** —
cellular automata, video feedback, speech feedback, iterative development. The
LLM version is the same shape: hack on the problem, analyze the errors, hack
some more, converge. The adventure compiler is the worked instance in this
repository, acting as a linter that tells the model what to fix on the next
pass. Each cycle is lossy and each cycle is better, and those two facts are not
in tension — that is what convergence *is*.

It is also continuous with a methodology Don has practiced, refined and written
about for decades, in NeWS and after: **interact locally, link globally.** Local
rules, iterated, producing global structure. Feedback loops are the point of the
architecture, not a leak in it.

**So if authorship diffuses and the result is a coherent, high-quality end
state, that is an acceptable trade — provided credit follows.** Credit to the
model, to the training, to the people whose work is in the training, and to the
iterative method itself. What is not acceptable is the diffusion happening
silently, which is a bookkeeping failure rather than an argument against
iterating.

**Provenance decays on each cycle, silently.** The guarantee that makes Case A
defensible is that any line can be checked against something the human wrote
before the machine saw it. After one round trip through a generator, some
propositions no longer have that ancestor. After two, nobody can tell which
ones do. The corpus becomes self-referential and the checkability condition has
expired without anyone noticing, because nothing about the text announces it.

The fix is bookkeeping, not restraint: **generation counter and origin, tracked
per piece.** Case A material is generation zero. Anything that passed through
Palm is generation one and is labeled so, forever, including when it re-enters
the corpus. Cheap to maintain up front, impossible to reconstruct later.

#### Where the combing metaphor applies, and where it does not

Combing a transcript to a shine is what Article VI forbids: **perfection is the
enemy of patina**, the tangles are the wear, and what the conditioner is
legitimately for is the hairballs — recognizer garbage, a false start carrying
no meaning. That is repairing the channel; the shine is composition, and it
removes the thing that proves a person was there.

But stated as a general law that would be wrong, and wrong in an expensive way.
**Oysters are entitled to produce pearls.** Iterating something to a shine is
how good artifacts get made, and the rhetorical convenience of "never polish"
is not worth what it costs to give up. The distinction is by **genre, not by
process:**

| | Iterate to a shine? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Testimony** — a comment, a post, a transcript attributed to a person speaking | No | The wear is the evidence. Polishing destroys what the reader is relying on |
| **Artifacts** — code, skills, designs, tools, interpreters, visualizers | Yes | Nobody wants a rough compiler. The pearl is the deliverable |

The reason this matters beyond the metaphor: applying Korzybski to LLMs has
already gone further than expected, and it goes further still with more code,
more skills, more tooling — interpreters, debuggers, explorers, visualizers,
direct-manipulation programming, simulation environments. All of that is built
by iterating, and all of it is artifact rather than testimony. **Do not throw
the baby out with the bathwater to win a point about polish.**

## The name is a liability, on purpose or otherwise

"-washing" is a concealment word: greenwashing, whitewashing, brainwashing. The
suffix asserts that something dirty is being made to look clean, which is the
opposite of what the practice does, since the laundered material is the author's
own signed corpus.

Adjacent terms it will be confused with:

- **AI washing** — overstating AI involvement, e.g. to investors.
  `verified: needs-check — believed to be SEC enforcement usage circa 2024`
- **Human washing** — passing AI output off as human work. This is the accusation
  voicewashing invites, and nearly the inverse of what it describes.
  `verified: needs-check — usage seen in marketing criticism, not well
  established`

So the coinage lands in a family of deception words while describing a
disclosure-maximalist practice. A hostile reader gets a free confession out of
the name alone. That may be the point — a term that concedes its own worst
reading has a certain credibility — but it will not defend itself, and anyone
adopting it should know they are carrying that.

## Open questions

- Does the practice need the term at all, or is "dictation off my own outline"
  sufficient and unattackable?
- What is the disclosure norm? Saying nothing seems wrong; a disclaimer on every
  post is noise. Is provenance a property of the artifact rather than the post?
- The outline is derived from public writing. What changes if any source is
  private, unpublished, or someone else's?
- At what corpus size does this become worth the setup? Ten posts, plainly not.
  A thousand, plainly yes.
- Is there a version where the outline is generated but never read — used only
  to sequence prompts the human answers, so even the ordering is disclosed as
  questions rather than absorbed as structure?
- Does Case B need its own name? It is a distinct practice — performing a
  character you authored — and borrowing "voicewashing" for it dissolves the
  condition that makes the term mean anything.
- If a Case B transcript re-enters the corpus and later feeds a Case A outline,
  is the result still Case A? The generation counter answers it mechanically:
  no. Is that the right answer, or too strict?

## Who to ask is a private question

Questions framed for named people are kept out of this file. A public design doc
that addresses individuals by name reads as a summons rather than an inquiry,
and the people worth asking deserve a message written by a person, not a
paragraph they discover in a repository.
