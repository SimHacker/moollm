# Computer Science Logo Style — Brian Harvey

Three volumes, MIT Press. The through-line from Papert's turtle to real CS.

**Read the books free:** [all three volumes online](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~bh/volumes.html)
at Brian's Berkeley page.

## The hook

Logo as a serious language for serious ideas — not a toy prefix on "learning to code." Volume by
volume: procedures, data structures, recursion, compilers — the curriculum Brian spent a career
making legible.

## Why Don loves it

Don's whole microworld stack — [SimCity/Micropolis](https://github.com/SimHacker/MicropolisCore),
pie menus, [MOOLLM skills](https://github.com/SimHacker/moollm) — assumes learners can
*reprogram the system they're inside*. Brian's books are the pedagogy that makes that assumption
honest instead of magical thinking.

## Show hooks

- What makes an idea teachable — Brian's answer across three volumes.
- Pair with Jens — the books explain *why* [first-class everything in Snap!](../snap-first-class-everything.md) matters for beginners.
- Logo → Scheme → Snap! lineage, told by the person who wrote the textbooks.
- Pair with Don's artifacts — C64 Logo Adventure (list/functional literacy, REPL-as-parser) and LLogo MacLISP rescue (where turtle pedagogy met ITS hardware).
