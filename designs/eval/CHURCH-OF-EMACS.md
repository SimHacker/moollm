# The Church of Emacs (St. IGNUcius)

*A sister religion, not a parish of the [Eval Genius](./CHURCH-OF-THE-EVAL-GENIUS.md). Same move: reverse-over-engineer a church from a technological artifact. The artifact is Emacs. The extra floors are St. IGNUcius. [God is in the Simulator Effect](./CHURCHES.md). The OS still boots without a halo.*

The other name next to Emacs is **St. IGNUcius** — Richard Stallman's satirical persona in the **Church of Emacs**. Pretend Intelligence is a late sermon of that church, not a second church. One nave, two centuries of joke.

## What it religifies

Emacs is a programmable editor. The Church of Emacs is reverse over-engineering *Emacs itself*: recover more design than the binary contained — a saint, a liturgy, a warning not to believe it. Stallman built the gap on purpose. "Hazardous to your health" is the Simulator Effect noticed from inside the pew.

MOOLLM's echo, without claiming the halo: inspectable skills, [cursor-mirror](../../skills/cursor-mirror/), [constitution](../../kernel/constitution-core.md) as operating law not mysticism. [P-HANDLE-K](../../kernel/constitution-core.md#8-safe-human-referencing-p-handle-k): we do not *be* Stallman. We cite the tradition.

## Pretend Intelligence — the 2026 sermon

RMS, Georgia Tech ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YDxPJs1EPS4), [event](https://calendar.gatech.edu/event/2026/01/23/free-talk-and-qa-richard-m-stallman), [LibreTech](https://ltc.gtorg.gatech.edu/2026/01/04/rms/)):

And if we start saying this more often, we might help overcome this marketing hype campaign that wants people to trust those systems, and trust their lives and all their activities to the control of those systems and the big companies that develop and control them.
