# Two ways of seeing, one of which names its eye

![A comparative plate contrasting Renaissance one-point perspective with Cubist simultaneous viewpoints, extended into architecture, theatre, literature and music](cubism-symmetric-dispatch.png)

**Plate for:** [*Case study: mystery as dispatch*](../README.md) — the section claiming Cubism is
symmetric dispatch · **Girders:** [`cubism-symmetric-dispatch.yml`](cubism-symmetric-dispatch.yml)

A Renaissance perspective construction is a **guard with one coordinate in it.** Every ruled line on
that panel converges on a single station point, and the picture is composed *to* that eye: stand
there and the tiled floor is a floor, the colonnade recedes, the architecture is true. Stand anywhere
else and the painting is quietly wrong, and politely does not mention it. The station point is not a
technique, it is a **binding** — the picture will only dispatch correctly for one receiver, and that
receiver's position is the thing the whole apparatus is built around.

Cubism keeps the object and throws away the privilege. The same jug, the same guitar, the same face,
gathered along several viewer coordinates *at once*, with no instruction about which one is the real
one — because the claim is that there isn't a real one, and that arranging a picture as though there
were is a category error about how seeing works.

Which is Korz's central claim, in paint, roughly twenty years before Korzybski named the error and
eighty before Ungar formalized it. No decomposition is the dominant one. You can gather along an axis
and get a view, and the view is real, and it is not *the* view.

**The best evidence that this is the same mechanism is the complaint.** The standing objection to
Cubism is *"it doesn't look like anything"* — which is, word for word in a different vocabulary, what
a Smalltalk programmer says the first time they are handed a sea of slots with no class to hold on to:

> **Where is the object?**

Gathered, if you ask along an axis. Not before. The discomfort is identical in both cases and it is
not naivety — it is the correct reaction to having a privilege withdrawn that you were relying on
without noticing.
