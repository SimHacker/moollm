# The same photograph, relit

![A theatrical diptych. Left: a 1930s drawing-room stage set almost entirely in shadow, five figures reduced to silhouettes, the table and clock face solid black. Right: the identical frame, identical figures in identical postures, under a full warm wash — faces readable, and a key, a note and a glass visible on the table. Brass plates beneath read WHODUNIT and HOWCATCHEM](whodunit-and-howcatchem.png)

**Plate for:** [*Case study: mystery as dispatch*](../README.md) — the mystery table, and

**Girders:** [`whodunit-and-howcatchem.yml`](whodunit-and-howcatchem.yml)

**Nothing moved.** That is the plate, and if you noticed it before reading this line the plate worked.
Same set, same camera, same five people standing in the same postures, same objects on the same table.
One variable changed, and it is the one the theatre has always treated as a variable: **the lighting
rig.** A stage is the right venue for this argument precisely because an audience already accepts
that the set is fixed and the light is a choice.

**Left is the shadowed pass.** Ninety percent of the frame is gone. You can read the architecture of
the room and nothing of its contents — the faces are unreadable and interchangeable, the table top is
black, the clock face is black. You are looking at a whodunit, and the reason it is a whodunit is not
that the author withheld anything. Everything is on the stage. **The coordinate you would need in
order to see it is simply unbound.**

**Right is the lit pass.** Now the faces resolve, the figure by the clock has an open and empty hand,
and there is a key, a folded note and a glass on the table. **None of that was added.** Those three
objects are in the left panel too, sitting in the black, which is the fair-play rule rendered as
exposure: the clue must be on the stage before the reveal, and the second pass has to be able to go
back and find it there.

So the same text, with no edit and nothing retracted, is two genres:

| | The question | Where the pleasure is |
|---|---|---|
| **Whodunit** | who | binding the culprit coordinate |
| **Howcatchem** | how, and how nearly | watching a bound mechanism operate |

**Which means a whodunit re-read is a howcatchem.** You know who before page one, so the question
the book was built around is answered on arrival and the interest relocates to the mechanism — how it
was done, how it was hidden, how close the detective came and when. That is Columbo's form exactly,
reached by re-reading Christie rather than by writing something different. **The genre changed while
the text stood still**, which is the best evidence in the essay that genre is a coordinate in the
reader's context rather than a property of the book.

**And it gives the test for whether a work survives at all.** A work is re-readable exactly when it
has slots guarded on a coordinate the first pass cannot bind. Guard everything on
position-in-the-plot and the second pass dispatches identically to the first — one surprise,
manufactured once, already spent. The layer a good book "serves you" the second time was always in
the dispatch table; the shadowed pass just could not satisfy its guard.

**Two is the floor, not the count.** The good ones keep paying past two, and they do it by three
different mechanisms that are worth keeping apart: a layer *uncovered* (this plate), a coordinate the
text *never binds at all* (Blade Runner's Deckard), and a mask you already carry being *fired* (any
line you can quote). Only the first is what "surviving the surprise" usually means.
