# Layers, and drawing tools in the loop — reading a real engine and taking it apart

*Part of the [Korz cauldron](../../README.md), sibling to
[margolus-rules.md](margolus-rules.md). **Spectrum: MOOLLM-integrated** — it reads a specific
piece of shipped software, [Don Hopkins's `CAM6.js`](../../../cellular-automata/cam6-cellular-automata-machine.md),
and argues about it. The mechanics stand alone; the diagnosis is about that codebase.*

**What it teaches:** how to tell when a naming convention is a missing dimension, why layers
inlined into engines produce the same bug five times, and how a paintbrush becomes an ordinary
stage in a cellular-automata pipeline instead of a thing that happens next to one.

The stack algebra and every line-number citation live in
[`layered-rules.yml`](layered-rules.yml). This page is the argument.

## Start by admiring the thing you're about to criticize

[`CAM6.js`](https://github.com/SimHacker/CAM6/blob/master/javascript/CAM6.js) is about 14,900
lines, and its whole extension architecture is one function:

```js
defineType(name, scope, indexGetFunction, indexKeys, objects)
```

Call it fifteen times and you get fifteen registries of plain dicts keyed by symbol:
`neighborhood`, `rule`, `colorMap`, `tool`, `analyzer`, `compositionFunction`, `image`,
`command`, `hint`, `chapter`, `lineCap`, `playMode`, `recordMode`, `tab`, and `paramMetaData`.
Every behavior in the system is a data structure with a symbol, a name, a description, and some
functions — listable, inspectable, savable. That is the yaml-jazz instinct arriving independently
in JavaScript, and it's why the engine can generate its own UI.

Three things in there are better than they look:

- **Rules and tools declare what they read.** Every rule dict carries `paramsUsed: {...}`, naming
  exactly which parameters it consults. That is a Korz **guard signature**, written down and then
  not dispatched on.
- **The front panel is generated by dispatch.** `paramMetaData` entries carry `type`, `scopes`,
  `widget`, `tab`, `recordable`, and — the good part — `condition: paramUsedByCurrentRule`. The
  panel that appears is a function of the rule that's running. CAM6 already computes its own
  controls from context.
- **`frob` is a bouncing slider.** `frob` seeks `frobTarget` at rate `unfrob`, scaled by
  `frobScale`. Value, target, speed: that is exactly Dave Tristram's Panel Library slider from
  [the prior-art section of Turn Tables](../../../cellular-automata/turn-tables.md), shipped inside a CA
  engine under a different name. The self-driving control was already there.

So the abstractions are right. What's wrong is the **enumeration**.

## Symptom one: the suffix is a dimension that never got to be one

Here is the rule registry, abbreviated:

```
Life          Life_Echo          Life_Heat
Brain         Brain_Echo         Brain_Heat
Eco           Eco_Echo           Eco_Heat
Margolus_HVGas    Margolus_HVGas_Echo    Margolus_HVGas_Heat
Margolus_Critters Margolus_Critters_Echo
VonNeumann_HGlass_Down   ..._Down_Echo   ..._Down_Heat
VonNeumann_HGlass_Up     ..._Up_Echo     ..._Up_Heat        (…and Left, and Right)
```

Every one is a separately maintained dict. Diff `Life` against `Life_Echo` and the entire
difference is `echoShift: 0` versus `echoShift: 1`. Diff against `Life_Heat` and it's
`heatShift` and `heatErrorShift` plus five entries in `paramsUsed`.

The same shape shows up in `recordMode`, more starkly. It has six independent booleans —
`recordingSnapshot`, `recordingScript`, `recordingCells`, `recordingParams`,
`recordingCommands`, `recordingTools` — and ten of the sixty-four combinations are hand-named.
The last one is called **`scriptCellsAllParametersCommandsTools`**. The name is a conjunction
read aloud.

**The tell:** when a symbol's name is a list of which features are switched on, those features
want to be coordinates. Korz's answer isn't clever here, it's just the obvious one: the user
picks a *point in a product space*, and nothing gets a name. `Life_Heat` becomes
`{rule: life, heat: {shift: 1, error_shift: 2}}` — and now the region between the named points
is reachable, which is the difference between a menu and an instrument.

## Symptom two: the layers live inside the engines

Echo and heat aren't components. They're `if (echoShift)` and `if (heatShift)` blocks
copy-pasted into the inner loop of *each* `neighborhoodFunction` — Life, Brain, Eco, Moore,
von Neumann. Five copies of the same fifteen lines.

Which is not stupid. It's **hand-run loop fusion**, and it really is faster than three passes
over the grid. There was no compiler to do it, so a person did it. The cost is that adding a
layer means editing every engine, and the intersection of *N* neighborhoods with *M* layers is
maintained by hand.

### What hand-fusion costs, concretely

⚠️ *Found by reading, not by running — worth ten minutes with the live app before anyone
believes me.* All five copies of the heat diffusion sum look like this:

```js
error += (( (nw & highMask) + (e & highMask) + (ne & highMask) +
            (w  & highMask) + ((c & mask) << heatShiftPollution) +
                              (e & highMask) +
            (sw & highMask) + (s & highMask) + (se & highMask) ) >> heatShift) + frob;
```

The top-middle term should be **`n`**. It's `e`, so north is never sampled and east is counted
twice. `n` is in scope and used correctly by the address assembler a few lines above, so it isn't
a naming problem — it looks like a typo that got copied four more times.

Predicted symptom: heat drifts east. And the comment sitting directly above the scan-rotation
switch says the four-rotation scan exists to cancel dithering artifacts *"that would cause the
heat to drift up and to the right."* Rudy Rucker caught the
original drift bug years ago; this may be a second one hiding comfortably behind the first one's
fix.

One typo, five copies. That's the architectural point, and it's why **fusion has to be the
compiler's job**: write the stages separately, declare that they fuse, let the machine inline
them. Same speed, one copy.

## The model: a generation is a pipeline, and `stage` is a dimension

```
source → neighborhood → rule → layer* → composite → measure → display
```

Each stage is a Korz sea — slots guarded over context. The interesting part is that **`stage` is
itself an ordinary context coordinate**, so a slot can guard on it. That single move is what lets
a paintbrush and a CA rule be the same kind of object sitting at different coordinates.

And every arrow between stages carries a **coherence decision**, the same axis
[margolus-rules.yml](margolus-rules.yml) raises for schedules: does this stage read the previous
frame, or what earlier stages already wrote this frame, or does it carry state from cell to cell
in scan order?

### The cell is a struct

A cell site is eight bits, and the bit layout is the real interface between layers. CAM6 does
this with `mask`, `highMask`, `echoShift`, `heatShift`, `toolMask` — the discipline is real, and
it's enforced by comments. Name the fields instead (`plane.life`, `plane.echo`, `plane.heat`,
`plane.opcode`, `plane.operand`), have each stage declare what it reads and writes, and
overlapping writes become a compile error instead of a visual mystery.

## The layers, written out

Full slot sets are in the [yml](layered-rules.yml). The shapes:

- **Echo** — pure per-cell copy of the past value into its own field. No neighborhood, no
  guards. The degenerate case of bank-and-selector: a routing chosen by a constant.
- **Heat** — diffusion with a dither, and the one that breaks the model open (below).
- **Life / anti-Life / Brain / Torben** — guards over an *aggregate* (`sum8`), so the residual
  table has nine entries and the compiler should generate a `popcount` rather than a table.
- **Anneal** — not a rule. An **arbiter**: a bank of two rules with a selector that is itself a
  rule (majority vote with the near-tie inverted). In CAM6 this is hand-woven into the `Eco`
  neighborhood function as an `if (anneal)` branch. Said out loud, "Life here, Brain there,
  boundary negotiated by a third CA" becomes a wiring diagram instead of a special engine.
- **RISCA** — the same bank-and-selector with the selector reading **cell content**: the high
  nybble names the layer, the low nybble is its operand. Programs are painted; adjacency is the
  calling convention; the eyedropper is a debugger. The bank must be open — sixteen is a bit
  width, not a design.

So four things that look like different subsystems are one component with different selectors.
[The Construction Set](../../../cellular-automata/cam-construction-set.md#one-pattern-four-scales)
argues this top-down from the phase matrix; this file arrives at it bottom-up from the slots,
which is a decent sign it's true.

### Heat is not a cellular automaton

Worth its own heading, because it constrains the whole back end. Heat's `error` variable
**accumulates across cells in scan order**. It's an error-diffusion dither — Floyd–Steinberg's
family — wearing a CA costume. Its output depends on iteration order, which is precisely why the
engine rotates the scan 0/90/180/270 on successive steps: to cancel the anisotropy over time.

A GPU port cannot keep that carry. So the layer has to **declare** `serial: scan`, and the back
end must either honor a serial pass or substitute a *declared* parallel dither — a per-cell hash,
or a blue-noise threshold texture. Silently parallelizing it doesn't make it faster, it makes it
a different rule. This is the first hard proof of
[iteration order as a plug-in](../../../cellular-automata/cam-construction-set.md#iteration-order-is-a-plug-in)
that comes from the *rules* rather than from the scheduler.

## Drawing tools: a tool is a rule whose neighborhood is the pointer

Now the question this file exists for.

A rule reads a neighborhood, consults guards, and writes masked bits into planes. A brush reads
`mouseX`/`mouseY`/`toolSize`, consults a region test, and writes `(toolCell & toolMask)` into the
same planes. Read, guard, emit — same shape. The only difference is where the neighborhood comes
from: the grid, or a hand.

**And CAM6 already did the hard half of this**, which is the part worth knowing before
redesigning it. The `line` tool doesn't touch cells directly. It draws with the ordinary Canvas
2D API — `moveTo`, `lineTo`, `lineWidth`, `lineCap` — into an offscreen **composition overlay**,
in white. Then a separately registered `compositionFunction` called `threshold_toolCell_set`
reads that canvas, thresholds a chosen channel between a min and a max, and writes
`(toolCell & toolMask) | (cells[i] & ~toolMask)`.

That is a **blend mode, registered as data**, with an arbitrary 2D drawing API as the mask
source. Meanwhile a second canvas — the **feedback overlay** — gets the same stroke in real
colormap colors, so the human sees the line while it's being dragged and before it's committed.
And `wraplicate` runs the whole tool nine times at ±width/±height so strokes wrap the torus.

The mechanism is right and under-generalized. Four fixes:

### 1. Where the tool runs is a dimension

CAM6 applies tools at one fixed point in the frame. Make `stage` a coordinate on tools like
everything else, because **the positions are genuinely different instruments**:

| Position | What drawing feels like |
|---|---|
| **before the rule** | The stroke is already one tick old when you see it. Drawing feels like *seeding*. |
| **after the rule** | The stroke survives the tick untouched. Drawing feels like *overwriting*. |
| **interleaved** | You paint into the buffer the rule is currently reading. The stroke starts propagating *during its own frame* — draw a wavefront and it moves off the head of the stroke while your hand is still moving. |
| **per substep** | For Margolus rules, between block phases. The only way to paint without breaking reversibility, because writes can be restricted to blocks not yet visited. |

This is the honest answer to "can drawing just be another composition step?" Yes — but the
composition step needs a **position**, and the position changes the physics, not the timing.

### 2. Source, region, and blend are three dimensions, not one tool

CAM6 has `squareBrush`, `circularBrush`, `squareSpray`, `circularSpray`, `squareRandomSpray`,
`circularRandomSpray`, `disruptor`, `line`. That's a cross product again, same as `Life_Echo`.
Split it:

- **Source** — a constant `toolCell`, an image (`WebCam`, `micropolis-tiles`, and the Stupid Fun
  Club logo are all already registered as `image` dicts), another plane of the same grid (an
  eyedropper that keeps dropping), another running engine (the layer bus — ganged CAM-6 cards),
  or a recorded stroke.
- **Region** — geometry, density (solid or sprayed, with volume and granularity), falloff.
- **Blend** — masked set, logical (`or`/`and`/`xor`/`andnot`), arithmetic (`add`/`max`/
  `average`), or **conditional**: write only where a guard over the destination cell holds, which
  makes a blend mode literally a slot set.

Eight tools collapse to three knobs, and combinations nobody hand-wrote start working —
spray-painting a webcam frame through an `xor`, for instance.

### 3. Wrap is one dimension, everywhere

`wraplicate: true` on a tool, the grid's toroidal edges, and a Turn Table's `wrap`/`bounce`/
`clamp` are three spellings of one choice. Declare `topology` once and let every stage consume
it: `wraplicate`'s nine copies are just 3^d for d=2 under a torus, a Klein bottle flips one axis,
and `clamp` needs one copy. The [boundary parameter that makes a slider
angular](../../../cellular-automata/turn-tables.md) is the same parameter that makes a grid a donut.

### 4. Preview is general

The feedback overlay is a preview of a pending write, and only tools get one. Make `preview` a
stage modifier — run the stage, show the result, don't commit — and you also get rule preview
(onion-skinning one tick before taking it), the machinery
[scrubbing needs](../../../cellular-automata/turn-tables.md) for dragging time backward through a
non-invertible rule, and measuring tools that are just brushes that never commit.

### Record and playback stop being a subsystem

If every stage is data, recording is a **tap on the stage bus** and playback is a **source
layer**. The six booleans of `recordMode` become four independent switches and a mode, chosen
rather than named. Better: a recorded stroke replays through the same source/region/blend path as
a live hand, so playback can't drift from live behavior — it isn't a separate code path to keep
in sync.

With one warning attached, because this repo has the receipt: **Electropaint** shipped to the
world as an SGI screensaver playing back recorded performances with the sliders hidden, and the
instrument became wallpaper
(the panel library page). Record and
playback are for teaching and for performance. They must never be the only way the thing is
experienced.

## What this demands of the compiler

The [yml](layered-rules.yml) lists it precisely. In short: stages as an ordered, guardable
dimension; named bit-fields with declared ownership; one bank-and-selector component; `defer_to`
delegation; **fusion**; a way for a stage to declare a serial dependency instead of having it
silently optimized away; topology as one dimension; blend modes as data; a general preview
modifier; and CAM6's generated-panel idea kept and pointed at the whole stack instead of only the
current rule.

The payoff is one sentence: **every rule name in `CAM6.js` that carries a suffix disappears**,
and the space between the names becomes reachable.
