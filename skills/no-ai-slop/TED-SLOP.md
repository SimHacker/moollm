# TED Slop — an indictment of insight porn

> **The feeling of a revelation, sold without the revelation.** A TED talk is food porn for
> ideas: you get the sensation of eating and skip the meal. You leave feeling smarter, more
> hopeful, slightly changed, and nothing has changed.

The companion to [no-ai-slop](SKILL.md) and [HUMAN-SLOP](HUMAN-SLOP.md). Models learned the TED
register from us; this file names it so neither side writes it. Recorded from a conversation with
Don, 5 October 2026, after he called out a reply for "sounding like a TED talk".

## What makes it grating

- **The arc is the product.** Personal hook, surprising statistic, reframe, "what if", standing
  ovation. Every talk on every subject has the same shape, because the format sells the feeling
  of insight, not the content. Once you see it you can't unsee it.
- **Hard problems get one idea.** Poverty, disease, education and climate each get solved in 18
  minutes by a single counterintuitive insight that needs no politics, money, conflict or years of
  boring work. Benjamin Bratton's TEDx talk, printed in the Guardian as "We need to talk about
  TED" (2013), called this placebo: it feels like treatment and isn't.
- **The speaker is the exhibit.** The real message is "this person is brilliant and you are lucky
  to be in the room." Humility is performed as setup for the reveal: "I was wrong, until..."
- **The audience buys participation.** People pay thousands to sit near ideas and leave with the
  identity of someone who cares about the future, without having built, fixed or risked anything.
  Applause stands in for action.
- **It launders status.** A stage, a red circle and a lanyard turn money into the look of thought.
  Anand Giridharadas's *Winners Take All* (2018) draws the line: "thought leaders" sell solutions
  that never threaten whoever pays for the conference; public intellectuals are allowed to say the
  system is the problem. Evgeny Morozov's "The Naked and the TED" (2012) said it about TED Books.
- **The makers become props.** The people who did the work, the grad students, the lab techs, the
  programmers, the nurses, appear as anecdotes in someone else's story. Credit flows to whoever
  tells it best.

## The Epstein class

The link is specific, not vibes. Jeffrey Epstein was a major funder of John Brockman's Edge.org
and its billionaires' dinners, a salon of scientists and tech money, and of the MIT Media Lab,
where the donations were concealed and Joi Ito resigned in 2019 when they came out. That world
runs on **proximity to brilliance**: the rich buy closeness to ideas and their makers, and the
makers get funding and access. When the currency is "I was in the room with geniuses", a predator
can buy the room, and the room's respectability with it.

The yappers and influencers are the same economy at retail: posing as makers by sitting near
makers, quoting the talk, sharing the clip, collecting the identity without the work.

## The same moves in prose, and the fix

| TED move | In writing | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| the reveal | "Here's the thing", "but here's where it gets interesting" | say the thing |
| awe as content | "amazing", "remarkable", "a game-changer" | say what it does, with a number |
| the arc | a list of facts staged as a story with a climax | facts in the order the reader needs them |
| flattering the audience | "you're part of something big", "imagine a world where" | the claim, and the evidence |
| one-idea solutions | a hard problem reframed into a slogan | the constraints, the trade-offs, what's still open |
| the speaker as exhibit | the writer's insight as the subject | the work, and who did it |
| applause lines | the bumper-sticker last sentence | stop when it's said |

**The test:** would a person who did the work write it this way, to another person who does the
work? A demo that runs beats a talk about the demo. Say what it is, show the evidence, credit who
did it, stop.
