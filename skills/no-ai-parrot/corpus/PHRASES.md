# PHRASES — the family of dismissals, and the arguments for them

The phrases themselves, what they descend from, and the considered defenses of
them. **The tell is never the phrase. It is whether a claim follows it.**

This file holds specimens and steelmen. It deliberately does **not** hold a
per-person catalog of who said what, when: the individual instances, the dated
chronologies and the thread-level analysis are kept privately, because the
argument should stand on the phrase rather than on anybody's posting history.
Ideas here are evidence-backed but not reactions to any particular thread.

Named authors who published an argument under their own byline are cited here,
because citing a novelist's public position is scholarship rather than dossier.

## The phrase family

One move, several skins. Harvested 2026-09-07 via the HN Algolia API; counts are
comment hits at that moment and will drift upward.

| Phrase | HN comments | Provenance |
|---|---|---|
| "stochastic parrot" | thousands | Bender, Gebru, McMillan-Major, Shmitchell 2021 — a real paper with a real argument, detached from it (see [`CANON.md`](CANON.md)) |

| "clanker" | rising fast | slur-form; carries no claim at all, which is the point of it |

The tell is not the phrase. It is whether a claim follows it. Compare the two
sections below: the same words do opposite work.

## Defenses worth answering

These are not clichés. They are arguments *for* the phrases, made by people who
thought about it, and the `STEELMAN` move is unserious if it invents a weaker
version than these.

### 2026-07-30 · Groxx — the pedagogical defense of "spicy autocomplete"

https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49104729

> I generally like it still. It describes their failure modes pretty well, and in
> a way that most people already recognize. [...] Practically every other attempt
> at describing them leans too technical and unfamiliar (stochastic parrot) or too
> anthropomorphic [...] You need to work from a reference that has both a shared
> understanding, and does not lead to problematic "if X has Y, and Z is like X,
> then Z has Y" seemingly-logical derived beliefs. "Spicy autocomplete" is a
> fairly safe starting point in both ways.

`kind: STEELMAN · verified: verbatim via HN Algolia API, elision marked ·
quote-limit: the tongue-in-mouth aside is funny but cut for length; quote it
whole or not at all if you use that part`

**This is the strongest case in the corpus for the other side, and it is not a
metaphysical claim at all — it is a claim about pedagogy and transfer error.**
Groxx argues the phrase is chosen *because* it blocks a bad inference. Any
answer that treats "spicy autocomplete" users as thoughtless has to explain
this comment, and cannot.

### 2026-09-03 · gwd — the phrase used correctly, as a named hypothesis

https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49548152

> In the same way, "non-aware spicy autocomplete" would produce an LLM that spoke
> about its own experience, based only on the input it has of people speaking
> about their own experience.

`kind: legitimate use · verified: verbatim via HN Algolia API`

The phrase in quotation marks, named as one hypothesis, weighed against a
competing one in the same comment, with the competing one given architectural
detail. This is what the family looks like when it is doing work. **Cite this
when accused of objecting to the words**: the objection is to the phrase used
as a verdict, and here is the same phrase used as a hypothesis, praised.
