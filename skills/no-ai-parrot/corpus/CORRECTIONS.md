# CORRECTIONS — good human answers, verbatim and attributed

The more useful half of the corpus, and the half that takes discipline to keep.
Clichés get saved because they sting. Good answers get lost because the moment
passes.

**Credit the author.** A stranger's sentence carries weight yours does not,
precisely because they are not the one who benefits from it. Quoting someone
else's better answer and naming them is stronger than restating it as your own.

**Entry requirement: a permalink.** Everything here can be pasted into a public
post, because a reader can click and confirm a named person said it on a date.
Good arguments with no public record are kept privately under a contract that
forbids pasting — see the privacy note in `GLANCE.yml`.

### DonHopkins · 2026-09-04 · the long-form version, and what it cost

> trained on linguistic form without direct access to communicative intent, it
> cannot possess meaning, understanding, or a model of the world.

> That distinction matters. The popular slogan discards the paper's detailed

reason is the middle quote: it concedes that the paper made the strong claim

complains about.

**The lesson the comment teaches by example, not by argument.** The same comment

simile and "so squawk for yourself," and then delivers the analysis and two
citations. **The analysis is quotable forever; the opening is why some readers
never reach it.** Extract the middle. That extraction is what this file is for.

## The convergence — "Determinishtic"

Not one good comment. **Five people in one thread, arriving separately at the
same architecture**, from opposite starting attitudes:

| Who | Where they started | Where they landed |
|---|---|---|
| chickensong | skills are useful | "express something deterministically with code" |
| ygjb | reporting shop practice | "the term used internally is to make things *Determinishtic*" |
| RicDan | "You don't" — unimpressed | "Must criterias need deterministic checks -> be it hooks or what not" |
| serf | answering the premise | skills fill "an unknowable blank" a model cannot |
| pletnes | skeptical of skills | "they already know" the documentation |

**ygjb's word is the one to adopt**, and it should be credited to him by name
every time it is used. It is better than "deterministic harness" because the
deliberate misspelling carries the qualifier: not deterministic, *Determinishtic*
— deterministic-ish, deterministic where it counts, the checks pinned down while
the exploration stays loose. A coinage that admits its own approximation is
doing honest work, and it is memorable, which matters more than precision for a
term that has to survive being repeated.

**Why the cluster is worth more than its parts.** A single agreeing comment is
one person's opinion. Five, including two who dislike skills, is a venue
converging on an answer — which makes it a coalition under Article IX rather
than a position to defend. The correct move is to *name what they already
agreed on and credit them for it*, not to argue.

The named term then becomes the link: **the post defines it and stands alone;
the skill is where it is worked out in depth.** Determinishtic points at
`robust-first`, `sister-script`, `skill-snitch` and the boot chain, which are
the same idea implemented — but a reader who never clicks has still received a
complete thought and a name for it.

The failure mode to avoid is the opposite of pursuit: **naming a term and
letting the link carry the content.** See Article IX — a link is not a reply.
