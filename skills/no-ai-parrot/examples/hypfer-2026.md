# Founding case: the same cliché, twice, seventeen days apart

One Hacker News account, one phrase, two unrelated threads. Round one was about
metaphysics and went badly for everyone. Round two was about workflow and is
winnable. Both are here verbatim because the value of this skill is that the
second occurrence should cost nothing.

All quoted text is public HN comment text.

---

## Round one — 20–21 August 2026

Thread: *Stop Anthropomorphizing Intermediate Tokens as Reasoning/Thinking Traces*
https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49360140

### The prompt

> Cyberpsychosis. A next token predictor is not a being.
>
> Stop posting these things. Stop thinking these things.

Note the structure: a metaphysical verdict, then a prohibition on the inquiry.
That is the strong form, and the prohibition is the part to answer.

Two comments later, to a different user who simply asked *"Why can't it be a
being? Why is thinking about such a possibilty bad?"*:

> Sorry, but if you're genuinely asking this not for trolling reasons then you
> should _really_ _really_ see a medical professional (not an insult).

PATHOLOGIZING, explicitly denied in the same sentence.

### The reply that worked

> You did too. And any machine that tried to deny that would have failed the
> Turing Test. And it's insulting to all of us for you to repeatedly claim and
> pretend that you did not.
>
> "There is no debate to be had here. Just a bad faith shouting match" is you
> constructing a cowardly exit.

Four sentences. Named the move, refused the exit, and the Turing reference does
actual work in the sentence instead of arriving as a citation.

### The reply that did not get read

The long version — the sign-flipped-sin argument, objectives are not ontologies,
Deep Blue and inclusive fitness, Minsky's *Why People Think Computers Can't*,
Society of Mind, Butler's *Erewhon*, Turing 1950 section 6, Jefferson's 1949
Lister Oration quoted at length with the solipsism passage — was the better
argument, and **HN's system filtered it as machine-written.** It never appeared
in the thread.

The opposing party found out only because he went looking, and said so:

> I saw that your reply got filtered by the system, googled your name, thought
> "huh", then tried vouching, but to no avail.

**This is the lesson the output constraints come from.** The most careful,
best-cited, most parallel prose in the thread tripped an automated
machine-detector, while the same venue failed all day to detect text that
actually was hybrid. The detector fails in both directions and your best writing
is what trips it. Write short, keep one unarmored line, hold the bibliography in
the skill.

### How it ended

> I think you might be operating in the mindset of a high-trust internet that no
> longer exists. You do not engage with sea lions, concern trolls, grifters,
> marketing people and the like. You shut them down.
>
> inb4 "who are you to decide that?" - "Me. I am me."
>
> That said, I appreciate what you did for computing at large, but you're missing
> the point here. This is (unfortunately) not a good faith debate club. The
> Internet as we know and love it is dead.

Two things to extract. First, "Me. I am me." is a claim of privileged being-ness
offered without any test, in a thread demanding tests of everyone else — and
"filter the participants for real human beings" is Turing's procedure smuggled in
as a door policy. Second, **recognition moved him where argument did not**, which
is worth knowing and is not a strategy anybody can reuse.

## Round two — 7 September 2026

Thread: *Ask HN: How do you manage skills files?* — a thread about organizing
markdown instruction files, with no metaphysics anywhere in it.

### The prompt

Replying to a user who described using skills to track commitments and follow-ups,
and who mentioned being on the autism spectrum with unmedicated ADHD:

> You're putting a lot of trust into the judgement abilities of what is just a
> next token predictor there.
>
> I can see what the goals are there, and they do make sense I suppose, but I'm
> not confident that what you're handing off there can be handed off to that
> degree.
>
> But maybe that is not the point and the point instead is to see what the LLM
> thinks would be correct, and then think about that and collect learnings about
> the world from it. It might not be right, but it still tells you how normal
> people think. So that's useful.

### What is different, and why round two is the winnable one

**The claim moved from ontological to empirical.** "Is not a being" cannot be
settled in a comment box. "Cannot be trusted with that degree of judgment" is a
workflow claim, and workflow claims lose to working artifacts. You never have to
touch the metaphysics.

**He concedes inside his own comment.** Paragraph three walks from *can't be
trusted* to *that's useful*. STEELMAN has already been performed by the
opposition; start from his own third paragraph.

**He is arguing against a position nobody in the thread holds.** The thread's
own answer, from four separate people, is that the model translates natural
language into deterministic tools and a script or hook enforces the sequence —
one commenter literally spells the house term as *"Determinishtic."* Nobody is
delegating judgment; they are delegating transcription and gating the result.

**His other comment is the good question**, and it is better than the cliché:

> Are there any "skills" at all that have proven to be useful? And if so, what's
> the context?
>
> Because, for me anyway, LLMs usually do one thing, and that then produces a
> durable artifact. So the prompt that got me there by that point expired and is
> not really needed anymore.

That is correct, and it is the design principle rather than an objection to it:
**the prompt is supposed to expire into a checked-in artifact.** His own fallback
— throwing commit IDs at it and saying "go do that again" — is a skill stored in
`git log`, keyed by a hash only he can find.

Answering the good question is worth more than winning the cliché. Do both, in
that order of effort.

## Extracted rules

1. **Same account plus same phrase in a new venue is a cache hit, not a new
   argument.** Look up the prior exchange first.
2. **Answer the empirical version, decline the metaphysical one.** You do not
   need a claim about understanding to defend a workflow.
3. **Find their concession and start there.** It is usually in their own last
   paragraph.
4. **Short survives; polished dies.** Length is a delivery risk, not a measure
   of rigor.
5. **When there is a good question in the same account's other comment, that is
   the reply that matters.** The cliché reply is maintenance; the good-question
   reply is the product.
