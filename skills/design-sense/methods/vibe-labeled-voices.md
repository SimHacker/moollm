# Vibe-Labeled Voices

**Class:** method · **Attribution:** Jeff Adkins (DnfJeff), local voice-AI
character work, 2026

> **Label every synthetic voice with the vibe it lands — so casting can match
> voice to role before a word is generated.**

Jeff Adkins' practice from running local voice-to-voice AI characters: his
voice inventory (preset voices plus 5–30-second clones) carries annotations
about the tone each voice actually lands with a listener — "this voice is
like a radio commercial," "this one would get old after a long time." The
labels aren't descriptions of the audio; they're **casting metadata**:
context and personality tied to the voice itself, kept separate from the
character, so dialogue generation can cast appropriately — and knows when a
voice's welcome will wear out.

The [media-equation](../lenses/media-equation.md) is what makes this
load-bearing rather than cosmetic: Nass showed listeners respond socially to
voice qualities — gender, dominance, energy, similarity to themselves —
involuntarily and immediately. Every synthetic voice is already making a
social claim; the only choice is whether it was cast or defaulted. The
radio-commercial voice really is one, to the listener's old brain, no matter
what the words say.

Practical notes from the founding use:

- **Separate voice from character** — the same character can recast (narrator
  voice for the intro, intimate voice for the confession); the same voice can
  serve many characters, if its vibe fits.
- **Track wear** — "would get old after a long time" is a real production
  constraint; label endurance, not just tone.
- **Casting is a dialogue-generation input** — the generator should know what
  voice will carry the line, the way a screenwriter writes for a cast actor.
