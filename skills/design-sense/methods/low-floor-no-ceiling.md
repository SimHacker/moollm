# Low Floor, No Ceiling

**Class:** method · **Attribution:** Seymour Papert (low floor, high ceiling); Mitchel Resnick (wide walls, Scratch); Brian Harvey & Jens Mönig (no ceiling, Snap!)

> **Easy to start is no excuse for impossible to grow.**

Papert's Logo standard: a low floor (a child can start) and a high ceiling (the
tool doesn't run out). Resnick added wide walls for Scratch — many kinds of
projects, not one staircase. Snap!'s claim is the sharpest: **no ceiling** —
because it never dumbs down, there's nothing to hit. Snap! is Scheme wearing
Scratch's clothing: first-class procedures, first-class lists, first-class blocks;
the beginner's drag-and-drop surface and the researcher's lambda are the same
language, so growth never requires migration to a "real" tool.

The design-sense use: when a tool's beginner mode is a separate, weaker language,
the ceiling is load-bearing and users will crash into it. Build the simple surface
*on* the powerful substrate ([sharp-substrate](sharp-substrate.md)),
not instead of it — stage magic for capability: simple view until complex truth.
