# Procedural Rhetoric

**Class:** method · **Attribution:** Ian Bogost (*Persuasive Games*, MIT Press 2007)
**Dispatch:** whole skill at moollm `skills/procedural-rhetoric/` — load it; this entry is the design-sense pointer.

> **Rules are arguments. Design them on purpose or they design themselves.**

Processes argue — Bogost called it "an unholy blend of Will Wright and Aristotle."
What a system measures, rewards, permits, forbids, and chains together is an
ideological claim, made in mechanics rather than words, and mechanics persuade
harder than words because the player *enacts* the argument instead of hearing it.

The method cuts both ways:

- **As author:** decide what your rules argue before someone else notices what they
  argue anyway. A wedding license implemented as a capability token argues about
  who marriage is for. A voting machine object argues that elections belong in the
  living room. A pool with no ladder argues something too.
- **As critic:** read any system — game, form, workflow, API — by what it makes
  easy, hard, and impossible. That's its actual position, whatever the copy says.
