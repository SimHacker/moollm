# Calm, Not Invisible

**Class:** lens · **Attribution:** Mark Weiser & John Seely Brown (calm technology); the correction: Ben Shneiderman, Don Hopkins

> **Calm means less interruption, not hidden state.**

Weiser and Brown's calm technology: the periphery informs without demanding
focus, and attention moves fluidly between edge and center — the technology
recedes *into skilled use*, the way a good pen does. The modern counterfeit
swaps that for invisibility: chrome hidden until hover, scrollbars that vanish,
gestures with no signifier, state suppressed for screenshot purity. That isn't
calm — it's the same interruption load plus a memory test.

The lens splits the two cleanly: calm reduces *demands on attention*; invisible
reduces *available information*. Visible state, reversible actions, and
predictable modes are what let the user stay in command while being interrupted
less — which was Weiser's actual definition. An interface can be quiet and
honest at the same time; the classical-HCI-vs-aesthetic-UI fight is mostly this
one confusion, weaponized.
