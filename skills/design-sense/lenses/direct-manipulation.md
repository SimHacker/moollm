# Direct Manipulation

**Class:** lens · **Attribution:** Ben Shneiderman (coined the term, 1982-83; HCIL)

> **Show the object. Act on the object. Undo the act.**

Shneiderman's three principles, still the sharpest test of an interactive surface:
continuous representation of the objects of interest; physical actions or labeled
button presses instead of complex syntax; rapid, incremental, reversible operations
whose effect on the object is immediately visible. Hands learn before the manual —
the interface teaches by being operated, and reversibility is what makes operating
it safe enough to learn from.

The lens in practice: when a design hides the object (state lives somewhere you
can't see), demands syntax (modes, incantations, memorized order), or makes actions
chunky and irreversible, direct manipulation names exactly which principle broke.
It's also the bridge lens — [dynamic-queries](dynamic-queries.md) is direct
manipulation at database scale, [gesture-space-constraints](gesture-space-constraints.md)
at multitouch scale, and the Sims-style world-as-menu is direct manipulation of the
[advertisement economy](../methods/advertisement-economy.md).
