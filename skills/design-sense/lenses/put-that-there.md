# Put That There

**Class:** lens · **Attribution:** Richard Bolt, MIT Architecture Machine Group (1980)

> **Let the finger resolve the pronoun.**

Bolt's demo — "put that there," said while pointing first at an object, then at a
place — is the type specimen of multimodal deixis: speech and gesture each doing
what they're best at, fused by timing. Words carry the verb and the intent;
pointing carries the reference, which words handle badly ("the third one from the
left, no, the other left"). Any interface that forces one channel to do the other's
job — speech spelling out coordinates, or gestures pantomiming verbs — is fighting
this lens.

Half a century later it's the design brief for voice assistants, AR, and
LLM-with-screen-context: the pronoun problem hasn't moved, and pointing (cursor,
gaze, selection, tap) is still how you solve it.
