# Mark-Ahead Suppression

**Class:** lens · **Attribution:** Don Hopkins (pie menus, DDJ 1991)

> **Self-reveal for learning; suppress for mastery.**

The pie menu's second-best trick: if the user's gesture completes before display
latency elapses, don't show the menu at all. The novice pauses, the menu pops up
and teaches; the expert flicks, and the very same stroke executes silently —
*mark ahead*. One mechanism, two faces, chosen by timing alone: no expert mode,
no preference toggle, no separate shortcut system to learn. The novice's slow
selection is literally a rehearsal of the expert's fast one
([self-revealing-gestures](self-revealing-gestures.md)), so the interface trains
its own obsolescence and then honors it.

The general lens: the best novice/expert split is *temporal, not modal* — measure
hesitation, not identity. Any UI that makes experts wait through the teaching
animation, or makes novices memorize before acting, has fused what timing could
separate. (Kurtenbach and Buxton later measured the same effect in marking menus;
the pies were spinning at Sun years before.)
