# Gutter Closure

**Class:** lens · **Attribution:** Scott McCloud (*Understanding Comics*, 1993)

> **The punch lands in the gap.**

McCloud's *closure*: comics deliver the blow between the panels. Panel one shows
the raised axe; panel two shows the scream over the skyline — and the reader
commits the murder in the gutter, which is why it hits harder than any drawing
could. "To kill a man between panels is to condemn him to a thousand deaths":
every reader executes it differently, and each execution is *theirs*, rendered
by the one artist whose style they never reject.

The design translation: what you omit, the audience builds better — but only if
the two panels are composed so the gap has exactly one emotionally-necessary
bridge. Closure is not vagueness; it's precision about what to leave out. Design
the white space, not only the panels: the cut in film, the ellipsis in prose,
the unshown consequence in a game, the log line that reports the state *before*
and *after* and lets the operator infer the event.
