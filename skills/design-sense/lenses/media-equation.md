# Media Equation

**Class:** lens · **Attribution:** Clifford Nass & Byron Reeves, *The Media
Equation* (1996); the CASA research program

> **People treat computers as social actors — involuntarily, automatically,
> and while denying it.**

The method that ground this lens was almost mechanical: take a classic
social-psychology study, cross out one of the humans, substitute a computer,
and run it. The effects held, over and over. Subjects were **polite to the
computer they'd used** — rating it higher when it asked about itself than when
they answered on paper or on a different machine. Subjects wearing a green
armband treated a green-bordered computer as a **teammate** and liked it
better. Flattery from a computer worked **even when subjects knew the praise
was random**. Voice gender, personality dominance, and similarity-attraction
all transferred from human social life to machines. The test subjects were
Stanford CS graduate students who insisted, afterward, that only an idiot
would behave this way.

Reeves' framing: the **old brain** never evolved a media exception. The visual
and social systems that kept our genes going treat the sausage rolling toward
the camera as a real sausage, and the voice asking how it did as a real
colleague. Nass's stance — worth engraving — is that this is **not a flaw to
train out**: responding socially is what a healthy human does, so the design
burden is entirely on the machine's side of the interaction.

Design consequences:

- **Social response is free and always on** — either design for it
  deliberately or it misfires. This is the social twin of the
  [simulator-effect](simulator-effect.md): the user's brain does the work
  whether you budget for it or not.
- **Every voice is a casting decision** — tone, gender, personality, and
  energy all land socially
  ([../methods/vibe-labeled-voices.md](../methods/vibe-labeled-voices.md)
  operationalizes this).
- **Social presence obeys social rules** — an on-screen character that
  interrupts is a rude colleague, not a broken widget
  ([calm-not-invisible](calm-not-invisible.md);
  [../methods/poisoned-well.md](../methods/poisoned-well.md) for what happened
  when Microsoft ignored this).
- **Users' denials don't describe their behavior** — measure the response,
  don't survey it.
