## Seed notes (grounding, kept for provenance)

- **media-equation** (lens) — People treat computers as social actors,
  involuntarily and while denying it. Nass & Reeves crossed out one human in
  social-psych studies, substituted a computer, and the effects held: subjects
  were polite to the computer they'd used (rated it higher to its face than on
  paper or on another machine), and armband-color "teammate" effects
  transferred to machines. The subjects were Stanford CS grad students who
  insisted only an idiot would do this. Design consequence: social response is
  free and always on — either design for it deliberately or it misfires.
  Sources: Nass & Reeves, *The Media Equation* (1996); the CASA (Computers Are
  Social Actors) research program; the Beyond Human transcript above.
- **poisoned-well** (method, cautionary) — A good principle shipped obnoxiously
  can poison its whole design space for decades. Microsoft Agent took Nass's
  research and delivered it as interruption plus impersonation (Clippy,
  BonziBuddy rode the same runtime); screen characters became a punchline and
  the space stayed radioactive until the LLM era. Corollary: study the
  infamous failures before re-entering a poisoned space — they explored the
  worst ways so you can steer clear. Sources:
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Agent ·
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BonziBuddy ·
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_Nass · Free The Sims ActiveX demo
  (prior art on the good path, killed by platform and permission):
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nzz0cFSmgiM
- **vibe-labeled-voices** (method) — Attribution: Jeff Adkins (DnfJeff). Label
  synthetic voices with the vibe they land ("radio commercial", "would get old
  after a long time") so dialogue generation can cast voice to role — context
  and personality tied to the voice as casting metadata, separate from the
  character. The media-equation makes this load-bearing: listeners respond
  socially to voice qualities whether you design for them or not.
