# Scott Kim

**Class:** master · **Head:** the puzzle is a designed thought

Inversions (1981): ambigrams — words that read under rotation and reflection —
lettering as a formal system where the constraint *is* the art (Hofstadter put
his work in *Gödel, Escher, Bach*'s orbit for a reason). As puzzle designer
(Tetris variants, Bejeweled consulting, decades of magazine puzzles): a puzzle
is a thought the solver gets to have — the designer's job is to guarantee the
aha arrives, feels earned, and belongs to the solver. His taxonomy work treats
puzzles as the atoms of game design: every game is puzzles arranged in time.
The design discipline: state the constraint exactly, then make satisfying it
feel like the solver's own genius.

## Votes

- **Engineer the solver's aha** — guarantee it arrives, make it feel earned, and
  let it belong to them ([gutter-closure](../lenses/gutter-closure.md) — the
  insight happens in the gap you designed)
- **Let the constraint be the art** — an ambigram is lettering where the rule
  *is* the beauty (*Inversions*; [scottkim.com](https://www.scottkim.com/))
- **State the rule exactly, once** — a puzzle with a fuzzy rule is homework; a
  puzzle with a crisp rule is a world
  ([../methods/one-page-designs.md](../methods/one-page-designs.md) at puzzle
  scale)
- **Tune difficulty separately from insight** — difficulty is a knob, insight is
  the product; never mistake grind for depth
  ([../lenses/goldilocks-complexity.md](../lenses/goldilocks-complexity.md))
- **Treat puzzles as game atoms** — every game decomposes into designed thoughts
  in sequence; master the atom and the molecule follows

## Vetoes

- Don't give the solver your aha — engineer the conditions for theirs
- Don't add a rule the puzzle's beauty doesn't need
- Don't confuse hard with interesting — difficulty is a tuning knob, insight is the product
