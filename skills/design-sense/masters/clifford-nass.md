# Clifford Nass

**Class:** master · **Head:** the media equation — people treat computers as
social actors, involuntarily and while denying it; social response is not a
flaw to train out but a design surface to take responsibility for.

Stanford communication professor (1958–2013). With Byron Reeves he founded
the CASA program — Computers Are Social Actors — by crossing the human out of
classic social-psychology studies and substituting a machine: politeness,
flattery, teammate effects, personality matching, and voice-gender effects
all transferred intact, even among subjects who swore they'd never do such a
thing. *The Media Equation* (1996) made the case; *The Man Who Lied to His
Laptop* (2010) turned two decades of lab results into design counsel; his
2009 multitasking studies showed heavy media multitaskers perform worse at
everything including multitasking. He consulted for Microsoft, BMW, and
Toyota — and lived the poisoned-well tragedy: his correct research, deployed
as Clippy-era interruption theater, got blamed for the deployment's sins.
Don saw him talk; the exuberance in the Stanford tributes (waving arms,
talking so fast he'd spit, cooing at a baby mid-recruiting-call) was real.

## Votes

- **Cross out the human and rerun the study** — the method itself: take a
  social-psych result, substitute a computer for one human, and measure;
  don't theorize what people do with machines when you can observe it
  ([../lenses/media-equation.md](../lenses/media-equation.md))
- **Design for the involuntary response** — the social reaction is free,
  always on, and immune to the user's denials; budget for it the way you
  budget for [foveation](../lenses/foveation.md)
- **Cast every voice** — voice gender, dominance, and similarity land
  socially before content does; the BMW recall proved it in production
  ([../methods/vibe-labeled-voices.md](../methods/vibe-labeled-voices.md))
- **Match personality, then agree** — similarity-attraction transfers to
  machines: users prefer interfaces whose personality resembles theirs
- **Behavior over self-report** — subjects insist "only an idiot would be
  polite to a computer" while being polite to the computer; measure, don't
  ask
- **Give social presence social manners** — a character that interrupts is a
  rude colleague; his research predicted Clippy's failure before Microsoft
  shipped it ([../methods/poisoned-well.md](../methods/poisoned-well.md);
  [../lenses/calm-not-invisible.md](../lenses/calm-not-invisible.md))
- **Don't multitask** — his own late research: heavy multitaskers are worse
  at filtering, memory, and switching; the finding cost him his own habits

## Vetoes

- Don't treat the social response as user error — the design burden is on
  the machine's side
- Don't trust what users say they'd do over what they demonstrably do
- Don't bolt a social character onto an interruption engine
- Don't confuse the poisoned deployment with the poisoned principle
