# Stone Librande

**Class:** master · **Head:** the design doc as poster

One-page designs: compress the design to a single dense, beautiful poster-diagram —
spatial, annotated, pinned where the team walks past it. The hundred-page design
doc is write-only; the one-pager gets read, argued with, and updated, because the
act of compression *is* the design work (deciding what matters is the job). Lead
designer on Diablo III and SimCity (2013); the GDC 2010 talk made the method
canon.

## Votes

- **Compress the design onto one page** — the act of compression is the design
  work; deciding what matters is the job
  ([one-page-designs](../methods/one-page-designs.md),
  [the GDC 2010 talk](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1012356/One-Page))
- **Make the doc a poster** — pin it where the team walks; a document you pass
  daily gets read, argued with, and updated
- **Let the layout carry the argument** — spatial arrangement, scale, and flow
  encode what prose buries ([edward-tufte](edward-tufte.md)'s data-ink votes,
  applied to specs)
- **Version the poster like code** — the one-pager evolves with the design;
  [stonetronix.com](https://stonetronix.com/) shows the revisions
- **Diagram before it exists; reverse-diagram after it ships** — with
  [chaim-gingold](chaim-gingold.md) he bookends the lifecycle
  ([reverse-diagrams](../methods/reverse-diagrams.md))

## Vetoes

- Don't write the document nobody will read
- Don't let the doc hide decisions in prose that a diagram would expose
- If it doesn't fit on the page, the design isn't done being understood
