# Stewart Brand

**Class:** master · **Head:** access to tools; pace layers

The Whole Earth Catalog's subtitle — *access to tools* — is a design philosophy in
three words: don't deliver experiences, deliver capability, and trust people with
it. How Buildings Learn: buildings improve by accretion and adaptation after the
architect leaves; design for the changes you can't predict ("all buildings are
predictions; all predictions are wrong"). Pace layers: healthy systems are
stratified by speed — fashion, commerce, infrastructure, governance, culture,
nature — with fast layers innovating and slow layers stabilizing; most design
disasters are one layer forced to move at another's speed. Co-founded The WELL
(the tourist policy as a business) and the Long Now Foundation, where the Eno
and Wright conversation lives.

## Votes

- **Hand over the tool** — access to tools is the whole philosophy; capability
  beats curation ([Whole Earth Catalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whole_Earth_Catalog);
  [../methods/tourist-policy.md](../methods/tourist-policy.md))
- **Design buildings that learn** — leave slack for the uses you can't predict;
  the tenants finish the architecture
  ([../methods/design-by-accretion.md](../methods/design-by-accretion.md),
  *How Buildings Learn*)
- **Stratify by pace** — fashion proposes, culture disposes; let fast layers
  innovate and slow layers stabilize, and never force one to move at the other's
  speed ([Pace Layering (JoDS, 2018)](https://jods.mitpress.mit.edu/pub/issue3-brand))
- **Ask what it wants to be in 10,000 years** — the Long Now question exposes
  which decisions are load-bearing ([longnow.org](https://longnow.org/);
  [brian-eno](brian-eno.md) named the Long Now)
- **Convene the scene** — the WELL, the hackers' conference, the catalog itself:
  the deliverable is the community that outlives it

## Vetoes

- Don't sell the experience when you could hand over the tool
- Don't force a slow layer to move at a fast layer's speed
- Don't finish a building — leave it able to learn
