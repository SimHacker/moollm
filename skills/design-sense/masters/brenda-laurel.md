# Brenda Laurel

**Class:** master · **Head:** the interface as theatre; designed experience

*Computers as Theatre*: the interface is not a conversation with a machine, it's a
stage where human and computer are co-actors in a shared action — design the
*action*, and dramatic theory (agency, engagement, catharsis, the shape of a
scene) becomes an engineering discipline. First-person experience over third-person
description. Purple Moon: research-driven design for an audience the industry
insisted didn't exist (girls), built from thousands of interviews rather than
assumptions. Placeholder: embodied VR narrative in 1993.

## Votes

- **Design the action, not the artifact** — human and computer are co-actors in a
  shared scene; dramatic structure is an engineering discipline
  (*Computers as Theatre*)
- **Give agency, then honor it** — the user's choices must matter to the shape of
  the whole, or the stage is a lie
  ([player-as-storyteller](../methods/player-as-storyteller.md))
- **Design first-person** — a body in the world beats a camera above it;
  Placeholder proved it in 1993
  ([Placeholder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placeholder_(virtual_reality)))
- **Research the audience you actually have** — Purple Moon interviewed thousands
  of girls the industry said didn't exist; design from field notes, not folklore
  ([cynthia-solomon](cynthia-solomon.md)'s classroom vote, aimed at market
  research)
- **Let catharsis in** — engagement has an arc; design the rising action and the
  release, not just the loop ([failure-as-entertainment](../methods/failure-as-entertainment.md)
  is comedy's version)

## Vetoes

- Don't design the dialogue box when the scene is the unit
- Don't mistake the audience you imagine for the audience you researched
- Don't give the user a camera when they came for a body
