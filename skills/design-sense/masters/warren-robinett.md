# Warren Robinett

**Class:** master · **Head:** the visible machine; the signed world

Rocky's Boots (1982): digital logic — AND, OR, NOT, flip-flops — as physical
objects a child drags and wires in a game world, with signal flow *animated
orange* so causality is watchable. The best exhibit ever built for "make the
invisible visible": kids built working circuits because the circuit was a place,
not a diagram. Adventure (Atari 2600, 1979): the first action-adventure — objects,
inventory, dragons — in 4KB; and inside it the first famous easter egg, his name
hidden in a secret room because Atari refused designers credit. The egg is design
rhetoric: the authored world signs itself, and the signature is *discovered*, not
displayed. Later: the NASA/UNC nanoManipulator — VR as a microscope you can touch.

## Votes

- **Animate the invisible** — orange means current: Rocky's Boots made signal
  flow watchable, and kids built real logic because causality had a color
  ([Rocky's Boots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky%27s_Boots);
  [../methods/everything-is-concrete.md](../methods/everything-is-concrete.md))
- **Make the circuit a place** — wiring AND-gates by walking parts around a game
  world beats any diagram; space is the teacher
  ([../methods/reverse-diagrams.md](../methods/reverse-diagrams.md) gone
  playable; [ken-kahn](ken-kahn.md) built the city version)
- **Fit the world in the cartridge** — Adventure put objects, inventory, and
  dragons in 4KB; scarcity forced the abstractions that defined a genre
  ([../lenses/masking.md](../lenses/masking.md) — the square knight works)
- **Sign the world from inside** — the easter egg is authorship asserted through
  the possibility space itself; discovered, not displayed
  ([warrenrobinett.com](https://www.warrenrobinett.com/))
- **Point the microscope somewhere real** — the nanoManipulator put VR hands on
  actual atoms; the display is a window, so open it onto matter
  ([ivan-sutherland](ivan-sutherland.md)'s ultimate display, cashed)

## Vetoes

- Don't teach with a diagram what a world could animate
- Don't let the medium's owner erase the author
- Don't hide the signal — orange means current, and the kid is watching
