# Scott McCloud

**Class:** master · **Head:** closure; amplification through simplification

*Understanding Comics* is the best interface design book that never mentions
interfaces. Closure: the reader commits the act in the gutter between panels —
what you omit, the audience builds better, and builds as *theirs*. Masking: the
simplified face is a mask the reader wears; detail it and the reader is evicted.
Amplification through simplification — stripping an image to essential meaning
amplifies that meaning. The whole book practices what it preaches: comics theory
delivered as comics.

## Votes

- **Leave the punch in the gutter** — the reader commits the act between the
  panels, and owns it ([gutter-closure](../lenses/gutter-closure.md))
- **Simplify the face, detail the world** — the abstract character is a mask the
  audience wears into a believable place ([masking](../lenses/masking.md);
  The Sims, Miis, and every anime background prove it)
- **Amplify through simplification** — strip the image to its meaning and the
  meaning gets louder ([simulator-effect](../lenses/simulator-effect.md) is the
  same bet in game clothes)
- **Practice what you explain in the medium you're explaining** — *Understanding
  Comics* is comics; the form is the credential
  ([explorable-explanations](../methods/explorable-explanations.md) kin)
- **Map the space before taking a position** — his triangle (reality, language,
  the picture plane) locates every style; make the map, then choose

## Vetoes

- Don't draw what the reader builds better
- Don't detail the avatar against a detailed world and wonder why nobody identifies
- Don't add rendering fidelity where meaning fidelity was the job
