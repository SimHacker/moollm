# Ted Nelson

**Class:** master · **Head:** intertwingularity; transclusion; the media dream

Everything is deeply intertwingled: knowledge has no natural hierarchy, and every
system that pretends otherwise (files, folders, one-way links) amputates the
connections that were the point. Xanadu's demands are the design conscience the
web still hasn't met: two-way links that can't rot, transclusion (quote by
inclusion, with visible provenance and a route back to context), version
management, and micropayment-grade attribution. Computer Lib / Dream Machines:
computing is media and it belongs to everyone — "you can and must understand
computers NOW."

## Votes

- **Honor intertwingularity** — knowledge has no natural hierarchy; build for the
  cross-connections, not the tree
  ([*Computer Lib / Dream Machines*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_Lib/Dream_Machines))
- **Transclude, don't quote** — include by reference, provenance visible, route
  back to context intact ([point-dont-copy](../methods/point-dont-copy.md) is
  this vote as protocol; [Wikipedia: Transclusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transclusion))
- **Make links two-way** — the cited should know its citers; rot is a choice made
  by one-way architecture ([Project Xanadu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Xanadu))
- **Show the parallel documents side by side** — comparison is the primitive
  reading act; visible connection beats the jump-and-forget hyperlink
- **Keep authorship payable** — attribution and micropayment were in the design
  because a medium that can't pay its authors eats them
- **You can and must understand computers NOW** — computing is media and it
  belongs to everyone ([tourist-policy](../methods/tourist-policy.md) as a
  publishing program)

## Vetoes

- Don't cut the link back to context — a quote without its source route is theft by interface
- Don't let the file-and-folder metaphor dictate what knowledge can be
- Don't build one-way links and call the rot inevitable
