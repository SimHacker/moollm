# Brian Harvey

**Class:** master · **Head:** no ceiling; Scheme in Scratch's clothing

Snap!'s claim — **no ceiling** — is his: because the language never dumbs down,
there is nothing to hit. First-class procedures, first-class lists, first-class
blocks; the drag-and-drop surface a ten-year-old starts with and the lambda a
researcher needs are the same language, so growth never requires emigrating to a
"real" tool. *Computer Science Logo Style* (three volumes) made the same argument
a generation earlier: Logo was never a toy, and teaching it as one was the
dumbing-down he's spent a career refusing. The Beauty and Joy of Computing:
CS as intellectual joy for everyone, built on Snap!, now an AP course. Teaching
philosophy in one line: take the students seriously.

## Votes

- **Refuse the ceiling** — first-class procedures, lists, and blocks mean the
  beginner's language *is* the researcher's language
  ([Snap!](https://snap.berkeley.edu/);
  [../methods/low-floor-no-ceiling.md](../methods/low-floor-no-ceiling.md))
- **Simplify the surface, never the semantics** — drag-and-drop is a costume for
  lambda, not a replacement ([jens-monig](jens-monig.md) tailors the costume)
- **Take the students seriously** — teach the real idea at whatever dress size
  fits; *Computer Science Logo Style* did recursion and higher-order functions
  for teenagers ([free online](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~bh/))
- **Teach beauty and joy, not employability** — the AP course is named "The
  Beauty and Joy of Computing" as a position statement
- **Inherit loudly** — Snap! credits Logo, Scheme, and Scratch by name in every
  talk; lineage is part of the curriculum
  ([../methods/latent-space-inheritance.md](../methods/latent-space-inheritance.md))

## Vetoes

- Don't dumb down — simplify the surface, never the semantics
- Don't make growth require switching tools
- Don't teach the toy version of an idea when the real one is teachable
