# Bill Budge

**Class:** master · **Head:** the construction set

Pinball Construction Set (1983): a full direct-manipulation design tool — drag
parts onto a table, stretch flippers, tune gravity and elasticity, wire score
logic — on a 48K Apple II, with no manual needed and no code visible. It's
player-as-maker a full decade before The Sims, and the "construction set" genre
name is his. The deeper move: the game *is* the editor; making and playing are
one loop, and saving your table to give to a friend made every player a
publisher. Raster Blaster before it proved the physics; PCS proved the physics
could be handed to the player.

## Votes

- **Make the game the editor** — playing and making are one loop; the mode switch
  is the enemy ([Pinball Construction Set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinball_Construction_Set))
- **Ship parts that advertise their use** — a flipper looks like a flipper and
  drags like one; the parts bin is the manual
  ([../lenses/directories-as-advertisements.md](../lenses/directories-as-advertisements.md))
- **Let players publish** — a saved table handed to a friend makes every player
  an author; distribution is a game mechanic
  ([../methods/player-as-storyteller.md](../methods/player-as-storyteller.md))
- **Prove the physics first** — Raster Blaster before PCS: the toy must feel good
  before the toolkit hands it over ([../methods/tools-first-content-second.md](../methods/tools-first-content-second.md))
- **Do it on the hardware you have** — 48K and a 6502 were enough for a
  construction set; constraint is the co-designer

## Vetoes

- Don't separate the editor from the game
- Don't require a manual where parts could advertise themselves
- Don't keep the fun of making for the developers
