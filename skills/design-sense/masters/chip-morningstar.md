# Chip Morningstar

**Class:** master · **Head:** detailed central planning is impossible; don't even try

Habitat's lead engineer and co-designer (Lucasfilm, 1986), and the man who put
the word "avatar" into computing. Co-author of "The Lessons of Lucasfilm's
Habitat" (1991), the founding postmortem of virtual-world design, whose first
commandment he wrote from experience: a world is defined by its inhabitants'
interactions, not its geometry, and the designers' detailed plans will not
survive contact with ten thousand users on Commodore 64s. The engineering
corollary: build the platform's *capabilities* right and let the events emerge —
which led him to Electric Communities and capability security (the E lineage),
where "who can do what to whom" is the world's real physics. Also the author of
"How to Deconstruct Almost Anything," the engineer's field report from an MLA
conference, which is design-sense applied to prose: jargon that gates rather
than points is a walled garden of its own.

## Votes

- **Define the world by its interactions** — inhabitants and their dealings are
  the world; geometry is scenery
  ([The Lessons of Lucasfilm's Habitat](http://www.fudco.com/chip/lessons.html))
- **Build capabilities, not policies** — make "who can do what to whom" the
  world's physics, enforced by construction
  ([point-dont-copy](../methods/point-dont-copy.md) — a capability is a pointer
  with teeth)
- **Plan the platform, improvise the events** — detailed central planning is
  impossible; ship the stage, let the play emerge
  ([tuned-emergence](../methods/tuned-emergence.md))
- **Run the small world first** — Habitat's beta taught more than any design
  review; populate early, watch closely
  ([play-learn-lift](../methods/play-learn-lift.md) at society scale)
- **Write the postmortem that stings** — the Lessons paper is worth more than the
  product; publish what surprised you
  ([How to Deconstruct Almost Anything](http://www.fudco.com/chip/deconstr.html)
  shows the same honesty aimed at prose)

## Vetoes

- Don't centrally plan what inhabitants will emergently author
- Don't confuse the world's geometry with the world
- Don't secure by policy what capabilities could secure by construction
