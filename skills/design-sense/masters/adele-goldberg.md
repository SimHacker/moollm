# Adele Goldberg

**Class:** master · **Head:** the system is only real when others can learn it

Co-author of "Personal Dynamic Media" with Kay, co-lead of PARC's Learning
Research Group, and the person who turned Smalltalk from a lab artifact into a
learnable system: the Smalltalk-80 books (*The Language and its Implementation*,
the "Blue Book") are the reason the ideas escaped the building. Her design
domain was the curriculum and the documentation as first-class system
components — a medium for children isn't done when it runs, it's done when a
teacher who wasn't in the room can teach it. Famously resisted showing Jobs the
goods in 1979 ("I said it was a terrible idea") — she understood what a demo
transfers, and what it doesn't.

## Votes

- **Write the books that let the ideas escape** — the Blue Book turned Smalltalk
  from lab artifact into a school; documentation is how systems reproduce
  (*Smalltalk-80: The Language and its Implementation*)
- **Treat curriculum as architecture** — the path into the system is a system
  component; design it with the same rigor
  ([../lenses/semantic-mipmap.md](../lenses/semantic-mipmap.md) — the Blue Book
  is a reading-order design)
- **Test the medium on the teacher who wasn't in the room** — transfer, not
  demo, is the acceptance test ([cynthia-solomon](cynthia-solomon.md) ran the
  same test in classrooms)
- **Guard the goods** — knowing what a demo gives away (and to whom) is part of
  stewarding a research program; she said no to Jobs and was right about what
  would happen
- **Co-author across the divide** — "Personal Dynamic Media" is Kay's vision
  *and* her learnability discipline; the pairing is the paper's strength

## Vetoes

- Don't call it a medium if only its builders can author in it
- Don't ship the system without the path into it — docs and curriculum are architecture
- Don't confuse the demo with the transfer
