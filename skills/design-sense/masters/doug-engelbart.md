# Doug Engelbart

**Class:** master · **Head:** augmentation; bootstrapping; collective IQ

Augmenting human intellect, not automating it away: the computer's job is to raise
the ceiling on what groups of humans can think together. Bootstrapping — use your
tools to improve your tools, and improve the improving (the ABC model: A does the
work, B improves A, C improves B). Tools and practices co-evolve; shipping a tool
without growing the practice around it ships half the system. The Mother of All
Demos (NLS, 1968) wasn't a product demo — it was a demonstration that the entire
medium could be different: hypertext, the mouse, live collaboration, video
conferencing, in one coherent augmentation system.

## Votes

- **Augment intellect; don't automate it away** — raise what humans can think
  together, not what machines can do alone
  ([Augmenting Human Intellect, 1962](https://www.dougengelbart.org/content/view/138/))
- **Bootstrap: use the tools to improve the tools** — and fund the C-work that
  improves the improving (the ABC model)
- **Co-evolve tool and practice** — a tool without a grown practice is half a
  system; train for powerful-in-use, not just easy-to-learn
  ([low-floor-no-ceiling](../methods/low-floor-no-ceiling.md)'s adult sibling)
- **Design for collective IQ** — the group is the user; NLS put shared documents,
  live collaboration, and video presence in one system in 1968
  ([The Mother of All Demos](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJDv-zdhzMY))
- **Chord the hard way when it pays forever** — the five-key chordset traded a
  learning curve for a lifetime of speed; sometimes the expert path deserves its
  own hardware ([mark-ahead-suppression](../lenses/mark-ahead-suppression.md) kin)

## Vetoes

- Don't automate what should be augmented
- Don't optimize A-work while starving B and C
- Don't design the tool without the practice — or measure it on novices only
  (easy-to-learn is not the same as powerful-in-use)
