# Dani Bunten Berry

**Class:** master · **Head:** games are social instruments

M.U.L.E. (1983): four players around one machine, an economy of scarcity,
auctions, and collusion — competition and cooperation deliberately entangled so
the table talks. Seven Cities of Gold (1984) coined "the fog of exploration" and
procedural continents. Her design compass never wavered: the computer is a
prop; the game happens *between the people*. "No one on their deathbed ever said
'I wish I had spent more time alone with my computer'" — carved over the door of
every multiplayer design since, honored mostly in the breach. She was designing
for the couch and the family room when the industry was designing for the
solitary teenager, and the industry is still catching up.

## Votes

- **Design the game between the people** — the computer is a prop; the product is
  what happens across the couch ([M.U.L.E.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.U.L.E.))
- **Entangle competition and cooperation** — M.U.L.E.'s auctions force rivals to
  trade; the tension is the fun ([../methods/possibility-space.md](../methods/possibility-space.md)
  for social states)
- **Keep the friction that makes people talk** — negotiation, collusion, and
  table banter are features; streamlining them out kills the game
- **Share the disasters** — a pirate raid on the common economy is everyone's
  story ([../methods/failure-as-entertainment.md](../methods/failure-as-entertainment.md))
- **Design for the family room** — four joysticks, one screen, all ages; the
  couch is an underserved platform in every era
  ([shigeru-miyamoto](shigeru-miyamoto.md)'s kyokan, [dan-gackle](dan-gackle.md)'s
  community-first instincts — same vote, different rooms)

## Vetoes

- Don't design the machine's game when the players' game is the product
- Don't remove the friction (auctions, trades, table talk) that makes people negotiate
- Don't mistake network play for social play — presence is the feature
