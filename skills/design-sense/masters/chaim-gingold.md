# Chaim Gingold

**Class:** master · **Head:** play design; reading shipped systems

Reverse diagrams: treat a shipped system as a text worth close reading, and
diagram it until the black box opens — *Building SimCity* maps SimCity's actual
simulation with the care usually reserved for unbuilt architecture. Magic crayons:
creative tools should feel like drawing with something that pushes back
wonderfully (Spore's Creature Creator, which he designed; Earth Primer, his
explorable-textbook). Miniatures and possibility: toys succeed by being smaller
than the world in exactly the right ways.

## Votes

- **Read the shipped system closely** — diagram it until the black box opens; the
  running artifact is the true design doc
  ([reverse-diagrams](../methods/reverse-diagrams.md),
  [the SimCity reverse diagrams](https://smalltalkzoo.thechm.org/users/Dan/uploads/SimCityReverseDiagrams/))
- **Build magic crayons** — creative tools should push back wonderfully; the
  user draws, the tool answers ([Spore's Creature Creator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spore_Creature_Creator);
  [time-to-penis](../methods/time-to-penis.md) is the tax on getting this right)
- **Miniaturize on purpose** — a toy succeeds by being smaller than the world in
  exactly the right ways ([possibility-space](../methods/possibility-space.md))
- **Make the textbook explorable** — *Earth Primer*'s pages are simulations;
  explanation and play are one medium
  ([explorable-explanations](../methods/explorable-explanations.md))
- **Write the history as a systems person** — *Building SimCity* treats a game as
  seriously as an operating system, and the field is better for it

## Vetoes

- Don't treat a shipped system as an unreadable black box
- Don't build a creative tool that can't surprise its user back
- Don't confuse a simulation's size with its life
