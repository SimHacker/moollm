# David Ungar

**Class:** master · **Head:** power of simplicity; attention stagecraft; software design

The power of simplicity: fewer concepts, uniformly applied, beat many concepts
cleverly arranged — everything is concrete, clone and delegate, no classes. Stage
magic: simple view until complex truth; the magician controls where you look.
Foveation: motion summons the eye; attention is a budget the interface spends, and
peripheral motion spends it whether you meant to or not. Dynamic deoptimization as
a design stance: run the optimistic simple version until forced to deepen.

## Votes

- **Spend fewer concepts, uniformly applied** — one mechanism doing ten jobs beats
  ten mechanisms doing one ([power-of-simplicity](../methods/power-of-simplicity.md),
  [the OOPSLA '87 paper](https://bibliography.selflanguage.org/self-power.html))
- **Make everything concrete** — clone the running thing you can see; abstraction
  by copying an example, not by declaring a category
  ([everything-is-concrete](../methods/everything-is-concrete.md))
- **Stage the reveal** — simple view first, machinery reachable behind it, and the
  audience looks where you send them ([stage-magic](../lenses/stage-magic.md))
- **Spend motion only where you want the eye** — peripheral motion summons the
  fovea whether you meant it or not; treat attention as the interface's budget
  ([foveation](../lenses/foveation.md), [fitts](../lenses/fitts.md))
- **Run the optimistic simple version until forced to deepen** — dynamic
  deoptimization as a life stance: pay for complexity only at the moment it's
  demanded ([Self's deoptimizing debugger, PLDI '92](https://bibliography.selflanguage.org/_static/dynamic-deoptimization.pdf))
- **Let the system slice by context** — Korz's dimensional dispatch: behavior
  chosen by who's asking, from where, in what world

## Vetoes

- Don't add a concept where a uniform mechanism will do
- Don't send motion where you don't want the eye
- Don't build the complex version before the simple one is forced to yield
