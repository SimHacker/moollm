# Cynthia Solomon

**Class:** master · **Head:** the child's epistemology comes first

Co-inventor of Logo (with Papert and Feurzeig, 1967) and the one who kept the
children in the room: she ran the first Logo classrooms, watched actual kids
meet actual turtles, and fed what happened back into the language — the
empirical loop that made Logo a medium instead of a syllabus. *Computer
Environments for Children* (1986) mapped the field's four cultures (drill,
discovery, eclectic, constructionist) with a practitioner's honesty about each.
The design stance: don't theorize the learner — observe one, then build for what
you saw. Later: Logo for the Apple II, the Atari research lab with turtles and
sprites, and decades of keeping constructionism honest about classrooms.

## Votes

- **Observe one real child before theorizing the learner** — the first Logo
  classrooms fed actual kid-turtle encounters back into the language design
  ([logothings](https://logothings.github.io/logothings/);
  [../methods/play-learn-lift.md](../methods/play-learn-lift.md) — watch the play
  before lifting)
- **Let the child program the computer** — never the reverse; agency is the
  entire point ([seymour-papert](seymour-papert.md))
- **Map the cultures honestly** — *Computer Environments for Children* names
  drill-and-practice, discovery, eclectic, and constructionist without cheap
  shots; know which one you're building
- **Make Tuesday morning the acceptance test** — the beautiful lab idea must
  survive a real classroom with real constraints
  ([adele-goldberg](adele-goldberg.md)'s transfer test, in school)
- **Keep the history** — she archives Logo's story because the lineage is
  curriculum too ([../methods/latent-space-inheritance.md](../methods/latent-space-inheritance.md))

## Vetoes

- Don't design the child's tool from the adult's theory
- Don't confuse teaching the computer with being taught by it — the child programs, full stop
- Don't skip the classroom — the lab's beautiful idea must survive Tuesday morning
