# Byron Reeves

**Class:** master · **Head:** the old brain never evolved a media exception —
visual and social systems treat screens as real life; and you learn what
media does to people by measuring what they actually watch, not what they
report.

Stanford communication professor; Clifford Nass's co-author on *The Media
Equation* (1996) and co-founder of the CASA program. His pre-Nass work
supplied the physiological half of the argument: brain activation climbing as
a sausage rolls toward the camera, because the old brain prepares for a real
approaching object — the systems that kept genes going for millions of years
process mediated experience as direct experience. Where Nass was the
exuberant front man, Reeves was the measured methodologist; his line on the
politeness studies — "then, you know, 50 studies later, we published a book"
— is the whole collaboration in one sentence. Later work carried the same
measure-don't-ask discipline forward: *Total Engagement* (2009, with Leighton
Read) on game mechanics in work, and the Human Screenome Project, which
records actual moment-to-moment screen use because self-reported "screen
time" is fiction.

## Votes

- **Run the old-brain test** — before theorizing a media effect, ask what the
  approach/avoidance, visual, and social systems make of the stimulus; the
  sausage is coming at your face whether you believe in it or not
  ([../lenses/media-equation.md](../lenses/media-equation.md))
- **Celebrate the social response** — his stance on the politeness findings:
  responding socially to media means you're a healthy human, not a fool; the
  reaction is the baseline, not the bug
- **Fifty studies, then the book** — one cute result is an anecdote; the
  media equation earned its name by replication across politeness, flattery,
  teams, personality, and voice
- **Measure the screenome, not the survey** — record actual screen behavior
  in situ; aggregate "screen time" self-reports obscure everything that
  matters
- **Mine games for their engagement machinery** — game mechanics are
  portable psychology; *Total Engagement* moved them into work before
  "gamification" flattened the idea
  ([../methods/advertisement-economy.md](../methods/advertisement-economy.md)
  is the same trick run in the other direction)

## Vetoes

- Don't trust self-report over recorded behavior
- Don't design as if audiences consciously evaluate media — the old brain
  answers first
- Don't publish the cute result before the replications
