# Henry Lieberman

**Class:** master · **Head:** your wish is my command; common sense in the loop

Programming by example's theorist and anthologist: *Your Wish Is My Command*
(2001) collected the field after Cypher's *Watch What I Do* founded it. Tinker
(1980s): one of the earliest PBE systems, teaching Lisp by concrete example.
Mondrian: graphical editing operations generalized from demonstrations. The
larger program: interfaces should meet users in *their* terms — hence the Open
Mind Common Sense project and ConceptNet with the MIT Media Lab, injecting
common-sense knowledge so software can guess sensibly what a demonstration
means. Prototype-and-delegation object systems too — his "Using Prototypical
Objects" (OOPSLA 1986) is a root citation of the Self lineage.

## Votes

- **Let examples be the notation** — end users are programmers whose language is
  demonstration (*Your Wish Is My Command*)
- **Put common sense in the loop** — a demonstration means something; ConceptNet
  and Open Mind exist so the system can guess *which* something
  ([../lenses/lean-into-latent.md](../lenses/lean-into-latent.md) — the LLM era
  cashed this check)
- **Share behavior through prototypes** — delegation to a concrete exemplar beats
  instantiating an abstract class
  (["Using Prototypical Objects" (OOPSLA 1986)](https://web.media.mit.edu/~lieber/Lieberary/OOP/Delegation/Delegation.html);
  [david-ungar](david-ungar.md) and [randall-smith](randall-smith.md) built the
  language)
- **Make the agent explain its generalization** — Mondrian showed what it
  abstracted from your edits; the inference must be inspectable
- **Aim tools at the 99%** — the people without a notation are the design brief,
  not an edge case ([../methods/low-floor-no-ceiling.md](../methods/low-floor-no-ceiling.md))

## Vetoes

- Don't make the user speak the machine's language when examples would do
- Don't generalize a demonstration without common sense about what it meant
- Don't underestimate end users — they're programmers without a notation
