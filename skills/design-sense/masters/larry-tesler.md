# Larry Tesler

**Class:** master · **Head:** no modes; conservation of complexity

Modelessness as a moral position: the same keystroke should never mean different
things depending on invisible state. His license plate read NOMODES, his website
was nomodes.com, and cut/copy/paste (with Tim Mott, in Gypsy at PARC) is what
modeless editing looks like when it wins — selection persists, verbs are
explicit, nothing lurks. Tesler's Law of the Conservation of Complexity: every
application has an irreducible amount of complexity, and the only question is who
swallows it — the user or the designer. He spent a career arguing it should be
the designer.

## Votes

- **Make the same input always mean the same thing** — modelessness is a promise
  the interface keeps ([nomodes.com](https://www.nomodes.com/);
  [calm-not-invisible](../lenses/calm-not-invisible.md))
- **Let selection persist and verbs be explicit** — select, then act: cut, copy,
  paste won because the object stays under the user's eye
  ([direct-manipulation](../lenses/direct-manipulation.md))
- **Swallow the complexity on the design side** — Tesler's Law: the irreducible
  complexity exists; the designer eats it so thousands of users don't
  ([Law of conservation of complexity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_conservation_of_complexity))
- **Test with real users, mercilessly** — Gypsy was validated on PARC secretaries,
  not PARC scientists; the CHI'88 spirit before CHI existed
  ([ben-shneiderman](ben-shneiderman.md)'s measurement vote, applied earlier)
- **If you must split novice and expert, split by timing, not by mode** —
  ([mark-ahead-suppression](../lenses/mark-ahead-suppression.md) is the modeless
  way to serve both)

## Vetoes

- Don't mode me in — no invisible state that changes what input means
- Don't push irreducible complexity onto the user because it was easier to ship
- Don't make the user remember what the screen could show
