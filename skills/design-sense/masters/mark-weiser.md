# Mark Weiser

**Class:** master · **Head:** ubiquitous computing; calm technology

"The most profound technologies are those that disappear" — but disappear like
good typography disappears: into the periphery, not into secrecy. Ubiquitous
computing (tabs, pads, boards at PARC) moved the computer out of the box and into
the world; calm technology (with John Seely Brown) is its ethic — engage the
periphery, inform without demanding focus, let attention move fluidly between
center and edge. The design-sense correction this skill carries: calm means less
*interruption*, never less *truth* — hiding state for screenshot purity is the
counterfeit of calm.

## Votes

- **Engage the periphery** — inform without demanding focus; let attention move
  fluidly between edge and center
  ([The Coming Age of Calm Technology](https://calmtech.com/papers/coming-age-calm-technology.html))
- **Disappear like typography disappears** — into skilled use, not into secrecy
  ([The Computer for the 21st Century](https://www.lri.fr/~mbl/Stanford/CS477/papers/Weiser-SciAm.pdf))
- **Size the computer to the task** — tabs, pads, boards: many computers per
  person, each at its natural scale, none demanding to be The Computer
- **Keep the user in command while interrupted less** — calm is about interruption
  budget, never about hiding state
  ([calm-not-invisible](../lenses/calm-not-invisible.md) carries the correction)
- **Prototype the world, not the demo** — PARC built and lived with hundreds of
  tabs and boards; inhabit the future to critique it
  ([everything-is-concrete](../methods/everything-is-concrete.md))

## Vetoes

- Don't demand the center when the periphery would serve
- Don't confuse invisible with illegible — the user stays in command
- Don't hide state and call it calm
